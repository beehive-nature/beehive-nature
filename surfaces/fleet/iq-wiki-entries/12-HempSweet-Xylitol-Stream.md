## Summary

Hemp hurd contains approximately **40% xylan** (a hemicellulose polymer of D-xylose). Through hydrolysis and conversion — via catalytic hydrogenation (nickel), microbial fermentation (bacteria), or engineered biological vectors (virus) — this xylose is transformed into **xylitol**, a premium natural sweetener with proven dental health benefits and diabetic-safe glycemic properties. HempSweet™ represents one of the highest value-per-kilogram product streams in the entire hemp biomass taxonomy, extracting premium biochemical value from what is currently treated as low-grade construction fill or animal bedding.

## 1. The Science: Hurd → Xylose → Xylitol

### 1.1 Input: Hemp Hurd Composition

| Component | % of Hurd | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Cellulose** | 40–50% | → Paper, bioplastics (Entry 06) |
| **Xylan (hemicellulose)** | **20–40%** | **→ XYLOSE → XYLITOL** ← THIS STREAM |
| **Lignin** | 20–28% | → Biochar, biocomposites |
| **Extractives/ash** | 2–5% | — |

> **Key Insight:** The ~40% xylan fraction in hurd is currently WASTED in most processing streams. HempSweet™ extracts this fraction and converts it into a product worth 10–50x more per kg than the hurd itself.

### 1.2 Step 1: Hydrolysis (Xylan → Xylose)

| Method | Process | Yield | Advantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dilute acid hydrolysis** | Hurd + dilute H₂SO₄ (0.5–2%) at 120–160°C | 70–90% xylose recovery | Fast, well-established industrial method |
| **Enzymatic hydrolysis** | Xylanase enzymes at 40–50°C | 60–85% | Mild conditions, no acid waste, "natural" |
| **Autohydrolysis** | Hot water/steam (160–200°C) | 50–75% | No chemicals added, auto-catalytic via acetic acid release |

**Output:** Xylose monomer solution (aqueous)

### 1.3 Step 2: Conversion (Xylose → Xylitol)

Three conversion pathways, each with distinct positioning:

#### Pathway A: Catalytic Hydrogenation (Nickel Catalyst)
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Xylose + H₂ gas + nickel catalyst → xylitol |
| **Conditions** | 80–140°C, 20–60 bar H₂ pressure |
| **Yield** | 85–98% conversion |
| **Catalyst** | Raney nickel or supported nickel (Ni/Al₂O₃) |
| **Advantage** | Highest yield, fastest, industrial standard |
| **Limitation** | Chemical process — nickel residues must be removed for food grade |
| **Best for** | Industrial-scale production |
| **Positioning** | Conventional xylitol — same as birch-derived |

#### Pathway B: Microbial Fermentation (Bacteria)
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Microorganisms metabolize xylose → xylitol via xylose reductase enzyme |
| **Organisms** | *Candida tropicalis*, *Debaryomyces hansenii*, *Pichia stipitis* (yeasts); engineered bacteria |
| **Conditions** | 28–35°C, pH 4–6, aerobic/microaerophilic, 24–96 hours |
| **Yield** | 60–90% (organism and conditions dependent) |
| **Advantage** | "Natural" fermentation — no chemical catalysts, consumer-friendly |
| **Limitation** | Slower, requires sterile conditions, lower throughput |
| **Best for** | Premium "naturally fermented" HempSweet™ branding |
| **Positioning** | 🏆 Organic / natural / clean-label positioning |

#### Pathway C: Engineered Biological Vectors (Virus)
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Engineered phage/viral delivery systems optimize microbial conversion efficiency |
| **Application** | Enhances bacterial pathway by delivering optimized enzyme genes (xylose reductase) to host cells |
| **Advantage** | Potentially highest efficiency biological route — engineered precision |
| **Status** | Frontier — R&D stage |
| **Positioning** | Next-generation biotech HempSweet™ |
| **BiGen Note:** | Requires evidence review for safety/regulatory approval [A/C] |

### 1.4 Step 3: Purification & Crystallization
- **Decolorization:** Activated carbon treatment
- **Deminerization:** Ion exchange chromatography (removes nickel residues from Pathway A)
- **Concentration:** Evaporation to syrup
- **Crystallization:** Cooling crystallization → xylitol crystals
- **Drying:** To <0.5% moisture
- **Food-grade certification:** FDA GRAS, USP/FCC standards
