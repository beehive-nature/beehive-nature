## Summary

Hemp hurd (also called shiv or shive) is the woody inner core of the hemp stalk, comprising 70–80% of stalk mass. Despite being historically treated as a waste byproduct of fiber processing, hurd is now recognized as a versatile industrial material with applications in sustainable construction (hempcrete), animal bedding, industrial absorbents, and engineered panels. Hurd's high cellulose content, exceptional absorbency, and porous structure make it uniquely suited for these applications.

## 1. Input: Hurd Anatomy

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Inner xylem core of stalk |
| **Mass fraction of stalk** | 70–80% |
| **Cellulose content** | 40–50% |
| **Lignin content** | 20–28% |
| **Hemicellulose** | 15–25% |
| **Bulk density** | 100–150 kg/m³ |
| **Absorbency** | 300–400% of own weight in liquids |
| **Key properties** | High absorbency, porous, lightweight, mineral-rich |

## 2. Processing

### 2.1 Extraction (Co-product of Decortication)
- **Source:** Separated from bast fiber during decortication (See Entry 03, §2.2)
- **No additional processing needed** — hurd is the "other half" of the stalk after fiber removal

### 2.2 Sizing & Screening
| Particle Size | Grade | Application |
|---|---|---|
| 2–10mm | H1 (Premium) | Hempcrete (optimal particle range) |
| 10–25mm | H1 (Coarse) | Hempcrete (structural), insulation |
| <2mm (fines) | H2 (Dust/fines) | Animal bedding premium, compost |
| Mixed | H2 (Standard) | General industrial, animal bedding |

### 2.3 Drying
- **Target moisture:** <15% for construction; <12% for bedding
- **Method:** Air drying or low-heat kiln (high heat degrades properties)

## 3. Product Streams

### 3.1 Hempcrete (Hemp-Lime Composite)
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Recipe** | Hurd + lime binder + water (typically 4:1 or 3:1 hurd:binder by volume) |
| **Applications** | Wall infill, insulation panels, floor screeds, roof insulation |
| **R-value** | ~R-2.5 to R-3.0 per inch |
| **Properties** | Carbon-negative, breathable, mold-resistant, fire-resistant, pest-resistant |
| **Lifespan** | 100+ years (petrifies over time — gets harder) |
| **Limitation** | Not load-bearing (requires structural frame) |
| **Market** | Green building, eco-construction; growing 15-20% CAGR in EU |
| **Carbon** | Sequesters ~110kg CO₂ per m³ of wall |

### 3.2 Animal Bedding
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Applications** | Horse, poultry, livestock, small animal bedding |
| **Properties** | 2x more absorbent than straw, 4x more than wood shavings |
| **Advantages** | Low dust, ammonia-binding, naturally pest-repellent, compostable |
| **Market** | Equestrian is largest segment; premium horse bedding commands high prices |
| **Used material** | Can be produced from post-extraction hurd (upcycling processing waste) |

### 3.3 Industrial Absorbents
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Applications** | Oil spill cleanup, chemical absorbents, hydromulch |
| **Properties** | Absorbs oil preferentially over water; biodegradable |
| **Advantage** | Natural alternative to synthetic absorbents (polypropylene) |
| **Market** | Environmental remediation, industrial maintenance |

### 3.4 Engineered Panels
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Process** | Hurd + binder → pressed panels (similar to particle board / OSB) |
| **Applications** | Furniture, construction boards, packaging |
| **Properties** | Lightweight, good insulation, workable like wood |
| **Market** | Sustainable building materials |

### 3.5 Mulch & Soil Amendment
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Applications** | Garden mulch, hydroseeding, erosion control |
| **Properties** | Moisture retention, slow nitrogen release, biodegradable |
| **Market** | Landscaping, agriculture, environmental restoration |
