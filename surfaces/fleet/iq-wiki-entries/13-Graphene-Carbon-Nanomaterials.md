## Summary

Hemp bast fiber can be converted into graphene-like carbon nanosheets through controlled thermal processing (pyrolysis/activation). Pioneering research at the University of Alberta demonstrated that hemp-derived carbon nanomaterials **outperform commercial graphene** in supercapacitor applications — at a fraction of the cost. This positions hemp as a transformative feedstock for the energy storage revolution.

---

## 1. The Science: Bast Fiber to Graphene-Like Carbon

### 1.1 Input: Hemp Bast Fiber Properties
| Property | Value | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Cellulose** | 70-85% | High carbon content = high yield carbonization |
| **Fiber structure** | Hollow, porous (lumen) | Natural porosity leads to high surface area carbon |
| **Hemicellulose** | 5-15% | Creates activation sites during pyrolysis |
| **Lignin** | 2-6% | Contributes to carbon yield |

### 1.2 Processing: Hydrothermal Carbonization + Activation

| Step | Process | Conditions | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hydrothermal** | Bast fiber + water, heated under pressure | 180-250C, 12-24 hours | Biochar precursor |
| **2. Carbonization** | Inert atmosphere pyrolysis | 600-1000C, N2/Ar atmosphere | Carbonized material |
| **3. Activation** | Chemical (KOH) or physical (CO2/steam) | 700-900C | Activated carbon nanosheets |
| **4. Exfoliation** | Thermal shock or chemical | Rapid heating 1000C+ | Graphene-like nanosheets |

### 1.3 Key Research Finding

> **Mitlin Group (University of Alberta):** Hemp-derived carbon nanosheets achieved surface area of 2,287 m2/g and specific capacitance of 106 F/g — **outperforming commercial graphene** at ~$500/kg production cost vs $2,000-20,000/kg for graphene from graphite. [A]
