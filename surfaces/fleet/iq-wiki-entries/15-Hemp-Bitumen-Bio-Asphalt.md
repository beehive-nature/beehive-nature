## Summary

Hemp lignin — a byproduct of cellulose and xylitol extraction — can replace petroleum-derived bitumen as the binding agent in asphalt road surfaces. Hemp bio-asphalt produces roads that are quieter, more flexible in extreme temperatures, and fully biodegradable at end-of-life. This stream transforms an extraction byproduct into a material that replaces one of the most petroleum-intensive products on Earth.

---

## 1. The Science: Lignin to Bitumen Replacement

### 1.1 Input: Hemp Lignin
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Source** | Byproduct of cellulose extraction (Entry 06) and xylitol extraction (Entry 12) |
| **Content in hurd** | 20-28% |
| **Content in bast fiber** | 2-6% |
| **Chemical structure** | Cross-linked phenolic polymer (similar binding properties to bitumen) |
| **Availability** | Currently burned as fuel or landfilled — FREE feedstock |

### 1.2 Why Lignin Works as Bitumen
- Bitumen is a heavy hydrocarbon BINDER that glues aggregate together
- Lignin is a natural PHENOLIC RESIN with similar binding properties
- Modified lignin achieves comparable viscosity, adhesion, and durability to petroleum bitumen
- Lignin is renewable, biodegradable, and carbon-negative

## 2. Processing

**Production:** Lignin (15-30%) + Aggregate (70-85%) -> Hot Mix (140-180C) -> Lay & Compact -> Bio-Asphalt Road

| Parameter | Hemp Bio-Asphalt | Petroleum Asphalt |
|---|---|---|
| **Binder source** | Hemp lignin (renewable) | Petroleum (finite) |
| **Durability** | Comparable (5-10+ years data emerging) | 10-15 years |
| **Flexibility** | Better in cold (less cracking) | Brittle in cold |
| **Noise reduction** | 2-5 dB quieter | Baseline |
| **End-of-life** | Biodegradable | Persistent pollutant |
| **Carbon** | Negative (sequesters carbon) | Positive (petroleum) |
