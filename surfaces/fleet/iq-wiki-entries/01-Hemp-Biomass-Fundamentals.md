## Summary

Industrial hemp (*Cannabis sativa L.*) is a cultivar of Cannabis sativa legally defined by a tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) concentration of ≤0.3% dry weight (USDA definition; thresholds vary by jurisdiction). Hemp biomass is the raw agricultural material harvested from hemp cultivation and serves as the input for the most diverse range of industrial product streams of any agricultural crop, spanning pharmaceuticals, construction materials, textiles, nutrition, energy, and advanced biomaterials.

## 2. Plant Anatomy & Biomass Components

The hemp plant yields **five distinct biomass components**, each feeding different product streams:

### 2.1 Flower / Inflorescence (Bud)
- **Mass fraction:** 5–15% of total dry biomass
- **Primary compounds:** Cannabinoids (CBD, CBG, CBC, CBN, trace THC), terpenes, flavonoids
- **Product streams:** Cannabinoid extracts (Entry 02); Terpene oils (Entry 07)
- **Harvest timing:** When trichomes turn milky/amber (60–90 days)

### 2.2 Bast Fiber (Phloem — outer stalk)
- **Mass fraction:** 20–30% of stalk
- **Primary properties:** High cellulose (70-85%), long fibers (5–55mm), high tensile strength
- **Product streams:** Textiles, composites, insulation (Entry 03)
- **Key metric:** Fiber length × tensile strength

### 2.3 Hurd / Shiv (Xylem — woody inner core)
- **Mass fraction:** 70–80% of stalk
- **Primary properties:** High cellulose (40-50%), high absorbency, porous
- **Product streams:** Hempcrete, bedding, absorbents (Entry 04)
- **Key metric:** Particle size distribution × absorbency coefficient

### 2.4 Seeds (Grain)
- **Mass fraction:** 500–1,500 lbs/acre (seed-type cultivars)
- **Primary compounds:** Protein (25-35%), oil (30-35%), essential fatty acids
- **Product streams:** Food, protein, oil (Entry 05)
- **Key metric:** Oil yield × protein content × omega 3:6 ratio

### 2.5 Leaves & Root Biomass
- **Mass fraction:** 10–20% of total (often left in field)
- **Primary compounds:** Trace cannabinoids, chlorophyll, minerals
- **Product streams:** Compost, biochar, phytoremediation residue (Entry 08)

## 4. Harvesting & Post-Harvest Processing

### 4.1 Cannabinoid Harvest
- **Method:** Hand or machine harvesting of flower
- **Drying:** 60–70°F, 45–55% humidity, 5–14 days
- **Curing:** Sealed containers, burped daily, 2–8 weeks
- **Storage:** Vacuum-sealed, <60°F, dark environment

### 4.2 Fiber Harvest
- **Method:** Cutting/swathing, then retting (dew, water, or enzymatic)
- **Retting duration:** 2–6 weeks (dew), 7–14 days (water), 24–72 hours (enzymatic)
- **Processing:** Decortication (mechanical separation of bast from hurd)
- **Storage:** Baled, dry, <12% moisture

### 4.3 Seed Harvest
- **Method:** Combine harvesting when 60–70% seed is mature
- **Drying:** To <9% moisture immediately (seeds oxidize rapidly)
- **Storage:** Cool, dry, sealed — seeds go rancid quickly if improperly stored

## 5. Why Hemp Is Unique

No other agricultural crop produces product streams across **eight major industrial categories**:

1. Pharmaceuticals (cannabinoid medicines) [A]
2. Construction (hempcrete, insulation) [B]
3. Textiles (fabric, rope) [B]
4. Nutrition (protein, oil, hearts) [A]
5. Advanced materials (bioplastics, nanocellulose) [B]
6. Energy (biofuel, biochar) [B]
7. Wellness (topicals, nutraceuticals) [B]
8. Environmental (phytoremediation, carbon sequestration) [A]

> **Key Insight:** The same plant that produces CBD medicine also produces building materials, textiles, food, fuel, and bioplastics. This multi-stream potential is what makes hemp biomass the most valuable agricultural commodity per acre on Earth when fully utilized.
