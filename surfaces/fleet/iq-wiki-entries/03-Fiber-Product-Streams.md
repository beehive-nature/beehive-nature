## Summary

Hemp bast fiber is one of the strongest and most durable natural fibers known, with a tensile strength exceeding that of cotton and flax. Extracted from the outer phloem layer of the hemp stalk, bast fiber feeds product streams including textiles, biocomposites, non-woven materials, rope, and thermal insulation. Hemp fiber represents the oldest industrial use of the Cannabis sativa plant, with archaeological evidence of hemp textiles dating back 8,000+ years.

## 1. Input: Bast Fiber Anatomy

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Outer layer of stalk (phloem/bark) |
| **Mass fraction of stalk** | 20–30% |
| **Fiber length** | 5–55 mm (avg 20–30 mm) |
| **Cellulose content** | 70–85% |
| **Tensile strength** | 550–900 MPa |
| **Microfibril angle** | 2–8° (low = high strength) |
| **Key advantage** | Anti-microbial, UV-resistant, highly breathable |

## 2. Processing: From Stalk to Fiber

### 2.1 Retting (Decomposition of Pectin Bonding)
| Method | Duration | Quality | Environmental Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dew retting** | 2–6 weeks | Variable | Low (natural) |
| **Water retting** | 7–14 days | Consistent, high quality | Moderate (water usage) |
| **Enzymatic retting** | 24–72 hours | Excellent, controlled | Low (enzyme cost) |
| **Chemical retting** | 1–3 hours | Fast but harsh | High (chemical waste) |

### 2.2 Decortication (Mechanical Separation)
- **Purpose:** Separate bast fiber from woody hurd
- **Equipment:** Decorticator (hammer mill, roller, or blade type)
- **Output:** Separated bast fiber + hurd (both valuable — See Entry 04)
- **Throughput:** 1–5 tons/hour (industrial)

### 2.3 Fiber Combing (Hackling)
- **Purpose:** Align fibers, remove short fibers (tow), grade by length
- **Output:** Line fiber (long, aligned) + tow (short, random)
- **Grades:** F1 (>40mm), F2 (20-40mm), F3 (<20mm tow)

## 3. Product Streams

### 3.1 Textiles & Fabrics
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Process** | Spinning (ring or rotor) → weaving/knitting |
| **Blend options** | 100% hemp, or blended with cotton, silk, Tencel |
| **Properties** | Anti-microbial, UV-resistant, breathable, softens with washing |
| **Fabric weight** | 150–400 g/m² (apparel to heavy-duty) |
| **Market** | Sustainable fashion, workwear, denim, home textiles |
| **Premium factor** | Organic certification, artisanal processing |

### 3.2 Biocomposites
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Process** | Fiber mat + bio-resin (or petrochemical resin) → compression molding |
| **Applications** | Automotive panels, construction boards, 3D printing filaments |
| **Properties** | High strength-to-weight ratio, impact resistance, lightweight |
| **Key advantage** | Replaces fiberglass with renewable, non-toxic material |
| **Market** | Automotive (BMW, Mercedes already use hemp composites), construction |

### 3.3 Non-Woven Materials
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Process** | Needle-punching or hydroentanglement |
| **Applications** | Geotextiles, erosion control, filtration, agricultural mats |
| **Properties** | Biodegradable, high absorbency, moldable |
| **Market** | Agriculture, construction, environmental engineering |

### 3.4 Rope & Cordage
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Properties** | Naturally resistant to rot, salt water, UV |
| **Applications** | Marine, agriculture, industrial, artisanal |
| **Market** | Niche but stable — hemp rope is the historical standard |

### 3.5 Thermal Insulation
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Thermal conductivity** | 0.038–0.040 W/m·K (competitive with fiberglass) |
| **Properties** | Moisture-regulating, non-toxic, carbon-negative |
| **Market** | Green building, eco-construction |

## 4. Quality Standards

| Grade | Fiber Length | Strength | Cleanliness | Primary Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | >40mm | >700 MPa | >95% pure fiber | Textiles, premium composites |
| **F2** | 20–40mm | 550–700 MPa | 85–95% | Non-wovens, insulation, composites |
| **F3 (tow)** | <20mm | Variable | Variable | Paper, padding, low-grade composites |
