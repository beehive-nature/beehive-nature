## Summary

Hemp is one of the richest cellulose sources in the plant kingdom, with whole-stalk cellulose content of 60–80%. This cellulose can be processed into bioplastics, paper, and nanocellulose — a cutting-edge biomaterial with properties exceeding many synthetic alternatives. Hemp cellulose products represent the frontier of the sustainable materials revolution, with applications spanning packaging, automotive, electronics, and construction.

## 1. Input: Hemp Cellulose Properties

| Source | Cellulose % | Hemicellulose % | Lignin % |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bast fiber** | 70–85% | 5–15% | 2–6% |
| **Hurd** | 40–50% | 15–25% | 20–28% |
| **Whole stalk** | 55–72% | 10–20% | 10–20% |
| **Comparison: wood pulp** | 40–50% | 20–30% | 20–30% |

> **Key advantage:** Hemp produces 2–3x more cellulose per acre than trees, with shorter growing cycles (120 days vs. 10–20 years).

## 2. Processing Methods

### 2.1 Pulping (Cellulose Extraction)
| Method | Yield | Quality | Environmental |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soda pulping** | 60–70% | Good | Moderate (chemical recovery) |
| **Kraft process** | 50–60% | High strength | High (sulfur emissions) |
| **Organosolv** | 55–65% | Very high | Low (organic solvents, recyclable) |
| **Mechanical** | 85–95% | Lower (high lignin) | Low energy |

### 2.2 Cellulose Isolation
- **Purpose:** Remove hemicellulose and lignin to obtain pure cellulose
- **Method:** Acid hydrolysis + alkaline extraction
- **Output:** α-cellulose >95% purity

### 2.3 Nanocellulose Production
- **Source:** Purified cellulose (from bast fiber — highest quality)
- **Method:** Acid hydrolysis (sulfuric acid) or mechanical fibrillation
- **Output:** Cellulose nanocrystals (CNC) or cellulose nanofibrils (CNF)

## 3. Product Streams

### 3.1 Bioplastics
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Types** | Cellulose acetate, cellulose-based composites, cellophane |
| **Process** | Cellulose → chemical modification → polymerization/molding |
| **Properties** | Biodegradable, compostable, renewable |
| **Applications** | Packaging, food containers, disposable products, 3D printing |
| **Key advantage** | Replaces petroleum-based plastics with carbon-negative alternative |
| **Market** | Bioplastics market growing 15–20% CAGR globally |

### 3.2 Paper & Packaging
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Properties** | Superior tensile strength, natural whiteness, longer fibers than wood |
| **Applications** | Specialty paper, banknotes, tea bags, cigarette paper, art paper, packaging |
| **Advantage** | Can be recycled 7–8× (wood paper: 3×) |
| **History** | First Bibles, maps, and early American documents were printed on hemp paper |
| **Market** | Niche/heritage today; growth potential as sustainable packaging |

### 3.3 Nanocellulose
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Types** | CNC (nanocrystals, 5–20nm × 100–500nm) and CNF (nanofibrils) |
| **Strength** | Tensile strength ~7,500–10,000 MPa (stronger than steel wire by weight) |
| **Applications** | Reinforcement composite, films/barriers, 3D bioprinting, electronics |
| **Properties** | Transparent, gas barrier, lightweight, biodegradable |
| **Market** | Frontier technology — projected $1–2 billion by 2030 |
| **Hemp advantage** | Hemp-derived CNC has higher crystallinity than wood-derived |
