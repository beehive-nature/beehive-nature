## Summary

Hemp biomass is a highly efficient feedstock for renewable energy production and carbon sequestration. Through processes including pyrolysis, fermentation, and anaerobic digestion, hemp biomass yields biofuels (biodiesel, bioethanol), biochar (a carbon-negative soil amendment), and biomass pellets for heat and power generation. Hemp's rapid growth cycle and high biomass yield per acre make it one of the most productive energy crops available, with the additional benefit of carbon sequestration.

## 1. Input: Hemp as Energy Feedstock

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Biomass yield** | 3–8 tons dry matter/acre (among highest of any crop) |
| **Calorific value** | 17–19 MJ/kg (comparable to wood) |
| **Cellulose content** | 55–72% (ideal for fermentation) |
| **Growth cycle** | 90–120 days (vs. 10–20 years for trees) |
| **Carbon sequestration** | 10–15 tons CO₂/acre/crop cycle |
| **Best input** | Whole-plant biomass (post-seed/flower extraction) |

## 2. Processing Methods

### 2.1 Pyrolysis (Thermal Decomposition in Absence of Oxygen)
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Temperature** | 400–700°C |
| **Outputs** | Bio-oil (30–50%), biochar (20–35%), syngas (20–40%) |
| **Key product** | Biochar — stable carbon for soil amendment |
| **Carbon impact** | Net-negative (carbon locked in biochar for centuries) |

### 2.2 Fermentation (Bioethanol)
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Process** | Cellulose hydrolysis → sugar fermentation → distillation |
| **Yield** | 250–400 L ethanol/ton biomass |
| **Advantage** | Cellulosic ethanol doesn't compete with food crops |
| **Challenge** | Pretreatment complexity for cellulose |

### 2.3 Transesterification (Biodiesel from Seed Oil)
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Input** | Hemp seed oil (See Entry 05) |
| **Process** | Oil + methanol + catalyst → biodiesel + glycerin |
| **Yield** | ~90% conversion efficiency |
| **Properties** | Cetane number ~55 (excellent); cold flow needs improvement |

### 2.4 Anaerobic Digestion (Biogas)
| Parameter | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Input** | Fresh hemp biomass (leaves, stalks, processing waste) |
| **Output** | Biogas (50–70% methane) + digestate (fertilizer) |
| **Yield** | 200–400 m³ biogas/ton biomass |

## 3. Product Streams

### 3.1 Biochar
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Form** | Porous black carbon solid |
| **Carbon content** | 65–90% |
| **Surface area** | 200–500 m²/g (highly porous) |
| **Stability** | 100–1,000+ years in soil (permanent carbon sink) |
| **Applications** | Soil amendment (water retention, nutrient holding), water filtration, animal feed additive |
| **Carbon credits** | Eligible for carbon offset certification [B] |
| **Market** | Premium biochar $1,000–3,000/ton; growing 15%+ CAGR |

### 3.2 Biodiesel
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Source** | Hemp seed oil → transesterification |
| **Yield** | ~300–400 L/acre (seed-type cultivars) |
| **Properties** | Cetane 55, density 0.88, excellent lubricity |
| **Market** | Biodiesel market mature; hemp as feedstock is emerging |

### 3.3 Bioethanol
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Source** | Cellulose from stalk/hurd → fermentation |
| **Yield** | ~750–1,200 L/acre |
| **Advantage** | Cellulosic — doesn't compete with food |
| **Market** | Advanced biofuels; policy-driven (RFS, RED II) |

### 3.4 Biomass Pellets
| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Form** | Compressed pellets (6–8mm diameter) |
| **Calorific value** | 17–19 MJ/kg |
| **Applications** | Residential heating, industrial boilers, power plants |
| **Advantage** | Pelletizes better than many agricultural residues |
| **Market** | Growing as coal replacement in EU power plants |
