the drop desk — publish a drop under informed consent. public code · public evidence · explicit uncertainty · execution within informed consent.

this desk publishes one post on the Hive blockchain, signed with a posting key, from this tab and nowhere else. every step shows you what it is doing before it does it: the post's bytes are hashed in front of you, the disclosure is on the page before the button, and the key never leaves this tab's memory. one click is the whole gesture — nothing here asks twice, and nothing here is a promise of safety: what was observed is what gets published.
one desk, one gesture: hash the bytes, read the disclosure, sign in-memory, broadcast, take the receipt. no gates, no double-asks — the click is the consent.
a static page that builds a Hive comment operation, signs it client-side (hive-tx, secp256k1, posting authority) and hands the signed transaction — never the key — to the RPC endpoint you name. the sha-256 of the body is computed locally and compared against the expected digest before you sign; the receipt re-fetches the landed body and byte-compares it. the wire is inspectable below at every step.

what this operation does: publishes the post above, irreversibly, on the Hive mainnet blockchain under the author named above, at the permlink named above.
what data it uses and reveals, to whom: the title, body and tags — to the public chain and every reader and front-end that ever mirrors it. the posting key — to no one: it signs in this tab and is wiped after the gesture; only the signed transaction is sent, to the RPC endpoint named below.
what the evidence establishes: the hash verdict above, and nothing more. acceptance of this disclosure never converts an UNVERIFIED finding into a proven one.
what remains uncertain: every scientific claim inside the body stays at its own evidence level; this desk certifies bytes, not conclusions.
cost and limits: resource credits from the author's Hive account; a post on Hive is free at point of use and capped near 64 KB — this desk warns past 60,000 bytes.
reversibility: none. a published drop can be edited only as a forked edition — the eternal law: preserve the edition, then preserve corrections against it.
