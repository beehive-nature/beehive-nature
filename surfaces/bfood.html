⬡ THE bFOOD HEXAGON

This is your food, drawn honestly. Tell it your height and weight, and every cell below fills up by how much of each nutrient today's food really gave you — not an average person, you. Where science doesn't know a number, the cell says so instead of pretending. That's the whole trick: nothing unmeasured is ever drawn as zero.
The food pyramid drew food groups and drew the same picture for every human alive. This draws the nutrients your body actually requires — one cell per nutrient, computed from your height, weight, age, sex and activity — and fills each cell by how much of it today's food really delivered. Nothing unmeasured is ever drawn as zero.
40 cells = the NASEM/IOM DRI set (EER via the 2023 equations, protein 0.8 g/kg, nine EAAs in mg/kg — the only body-scaled rows) against USDA FoodData Central composition, per record ID, refetchable. Three render states, never collapsed: measured (solid rim) · NotMeasured (dashed, empty, n/m — never zero) · floor (dashed, filled, ≥ — a sum over foods missing a published row is a lower bound wearing a total's name). 1 · YOUR BODY — the five inputs that genuinely move a requirement

Only these four scale with your body, and this page will not pretend otherwise. Energy uses the NASEM 2023 EER equation, where your height, weight, age and activity are the variables. Protein is an RDA in grams per kilogram, so your mass is the number. Fibre scales through energy at 14 g per 1,000 kcal. Every vitamin and mineral below is a flat constant for your age and sex group — NASEM uses reference weights inside the committee's derivation, never as a coefficient in the published value. Inventing a weight curve for zinc to make this page feel more personal would be the exact fake precision this project exists to refuse, so the cells say 👥 group and mean it.

5 · PROTEIN QUALITY — why 31.6 grams of protein is not 31.6 grams of protein

This is the fourth time this project has caught the same error class, and this time it is caught on our own favourite food. A lab's "total cannabinoids" is a sum over four analytes wearing a total's name. "Saturated fat" is two things a body routes differently, collapsed into one row. A basket total is a floor when a food has no published row. And a food's protein grams are an upper bound on usable protein, not a measurement of it.

THE MECHANISM — established biochemistry, not an outcome claim

A ribosome reading an mRNA needs the exact amino acid its codon calls for, at the moment it reads it. There is no substitution and no skipping. So the number of complete proteins a meal can build is capped by whichever indispensable amino acid runs out first relative to need — the limiting amino acid. And humans have no storage depot for amino acids: nothing like glycogen for glucose. The surplus cannot be banked. It is deaminated in the liver, its nitrogen fixed into urea and excreted, and its carbon skeleton burned or stored as fat. The excess still delivers calories. It delivers no protein.

AND THE BRAKE, which matters more than the finding

The limiting amino acid is a property of the whole day's amino acid supply, not of any single food. The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, current position: "The terms complete and incomplete are misleading in relation to plant protein. Protein from a variety of plant foods, eaten during the course of a day, supplies enough of all indispensable amino acids when caloric requirements are met." During the course of a day — not at the same meal. That "combine at every meal" rule was retracted decades ago. So this page will never print an "incomplete protein" warning beside a food: it would be mechanically derived and substantively false.

Hemp's first limiting amino acid is lysine — and it is lysine for every cereal and seed protein, while it is methionine+cysteine for every legume. That mismatch is exactly why complementation works: two proteins limited by different things, pooled, are limited by neither. It is not a trick, it is arithmetic on the aggregate.

✓ AND THE HEXAGON REPRODUCES IT INDEPENDENTLY — which is the only reason you should believe either

The claim above comes from published feeding studies. The nine amino acid cells in section 2 come from somewhere else entirely: raw USDA composition, divided by requirements read out of the DRI report. Two routes that share no data. At the default basket they land on the same answer — lysine is the lowest cell of the nine, at roughly half your requirement, while the other eight run from 79% to 123%. Nobody told the arithmetic what to find. If the two had disagreed, this section would say so instead, because a page that only reports its confirmations is not measuring anything.

Read the whiskers, not the bars. Those are one standard deviation, and for egg (±58) and whey (±25) they are enormous — the review's own conclusion is that variation within a protein type, driven by processing and genotype, is large. A single point value for "egg" or "whey" would be the misleading thing here, so the spread is drawn. Two further cautions we have to state against ourselves: these are scored against the 0.5–3 year reference pattern, and a different pattern moves every score; and whey scoring 85 with histidine limiting will surprise anyone expecting whey above 100 — that is the pattern choice plus real between-study variance, not an error.

6 · FIBRE — the outcome evidence, carrying the certainty its own authors assigned

A correction this page owes on its own hero food first. Hemp's fibre is overwhelmingly in the hull — hulls run 77–84% dietary fibre, whole seed 28–36%. Hearts are the dehulled fraction, and measure 4.0 g per 100 g. Whole-seed fibre figures must never be quoted for hearts, and this page uses the hearts number. Hemp hearts are an excellent protein and fat source and a poor fibre source; saying so costs nothing but a slogan.
Below is the largest synthesis in the field — Reynolds & Mann et al., The Lancet 2019: 185 prospective publications, just under 135 million person-years, plus 58 randomised trials, commissioned by WHO. The certainty column is the authors' own GRADE rating, not ours, and it is the most important column.

THE TENSION YOU MUST SEE, and it is the whole reason this table is drawn this way. Look down the two columns together. Every disease endpoint is observational — no trial has ever randomised people to high or low fibre and counted deaths — so the 15–30% mortality reductions are associations, open to the obvious confound that people who eat more fibre differ in smoking, activity, income and whole diet. And every outcome with High or Moderate certainty has a small effect: 0.37 kg of body weight, 1.27 mmHg of blood pressure whose confidence interval barely clears zero. The outcomes with big effects have the weakest evidence; the outcomes with the strongest evidence have small effects. A reader who takes "HIGH certainty" to mean "large benefit" has been misled, and this page will not be the thing that misleads them. The authors themselves write only that the relationships "may be" causal.
Two limits that bear directly on this page's own interests. First: the review states plainly that "limited data were available regarding specific sources or subcategories of dietary fibre" — the signal is dominated by cereal fibre. Hemp fibre cannot inherit these results, and this page does not let it. Second: the trials excluded powdered fibre supplements by design, and the authors note supplemental-fibre reviews show "only small effects." This evidence supports fibre-rich foods, not isolated fibre — which cuts against every product this project might one day want to sell. Also recorded: the authors name one harm, that high intakes may be deleterious where iron or mineral status is already borderline. And the optimum they identify is 25–29 g/day, against typical population intakes below 20.
What is mechanism and what is outcome. That colonic fermentation yields acetate, propionate and butyrate, and that butyrate is the primary fuel of the colonocyte, is established biochemistry. That any specific clinical outcome in the table above is mediated by those short-chain fatty acids in humans is a plausible and much-studied hypothesis, not a demonstrated causal chain. Mechanism and outcome are joined here by inference. That seam is where this page stops and the graded evidence deck begins.

7 · FAT, DISAGGREGATED — "saturated fat" is never one number here

That category collapses two things a body routes differently — MCTs (C6–C12) leave the gut by the portal vein straight to the liver, LCTs (C14+) by chylomicron through the lymphatics. USDA publishes fatty acids individually by chain length, so this split is not an interpretation we add; it is a column most labels stop showing. Lauric acid (C12) gets its own row because it is an MCT by chain length yet is absorbed substantially like an LCT — and coconut oil, this basket's own MCT source, is roughly half lauric. Publishing that against our own favourite fat is the point: a page that only disaggregates other people's food is marketing.

8 · THE ARBITRAGE — what the measurements say people actually miss

THE BRAKE, and it ships with the number every time. "Below the EAR" is a statistic about dietary intake, not a deficiency rate. Measured by serum 25(OH)D, about 5% of people are at risk of vitamin D deficiency against 96% below the dietary EAR — because the board set that RDA "on the assumption that people receive minimal sun exposure." Vitamin E intake is under-captured because cooking fats go uncounted, and choline has no EAR at all. Publishing 96 / 88 / 74% without this paragraph would be technically accurate and substantively misleading, which is the failure mode this page exists to refuse.
And the half that never gets quoted: selenium, niacin, riboflavin, phosphorus, iron (6%), thiamin, B12 and protein are not population shortfalls. A page that shows only the alarming half is a campaign, not a measurement.

9 · THE QUEST — a plan for a day, a week, a month, a year

Each rung is a real span of time and a different strength of evidence. You cannot claim a year-scale result from a day of remembering what you ate, so the rungs do not let you. Every quest is built and checked in Beehive University.

…AND HOW ANY OF IT COULD BE BELIEVED

Four different questions hide inside "how do we know it worked", and they have four different answers. Collapsing them is how wellness claims get made.
