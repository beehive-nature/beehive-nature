⬡ THE bFOOD HEXAGON

The food pyramid drew food groups and drew the same picture for every human alive. This draws the nutrients your body actually requires — one cell per nutrient, computed from your height, weight, age, sex and activity — and fills each cell by how much of it today's food really delivered. Nothing unmeasured is ever drawn as zero.
—

1 · YOUR BODY — the five inputs that genuinely move a requirement

Only these four scale with your body, and this page will not pretend otherwise. Energy uses the NASEM 2023 EER equation, where your height, weight, age and activity are the variables. Protein is an RDA in grams per kilogram, so your mass is the number. Fibre scales through energy at 14 g per 1,000 kcal. Every vitamin and mineral below is a flat constant for your age and sex group — NASEM uses reference weights inside the committee's derivation, never as a coefficient in the published value. Inventing a weight curve for zinc to make this page feel more personal would be the exact fake precision this project exists to refuse, so the cells say 👥 group and mean it.

3 · THE BASKET — three honest weights, never one implied

The house serving is 1.25 g of hemp hearts per kg of body weight — 100 g for an 80 kg adult, founder-set, and labelled a house basis rather than a published standard, because it is one. It moves when your weight moves. The reference intakes beside it do not. Two numbers that share a unit but not a pedigree must never be allowed to touch.

4 · THE LEDGER — every nutrient, the exact amount, and where it came from

Sorted by how well the basket covers it. The tick on each bar is 100% of your requirement. ≥ means a food in your basket has no published row for that nutrient, so the total is a floor, not a total — the same law that forbids a lab's four-analyte panel from calling itself "total cannabinoids".

5 · FAT, DISAGGREGATED — "saturated fat" is never one number here

That category collapses two things a body routes differently — MCTs (C6–C12) leave the gut by the portal vein straight to the liver, LCTs (C14+) by chylomicron through the lymphatics. USDA publishes fatty acids individually by chain length, so this split is not an interpretation we add; it is a column most labels stop showing. Lauric acid (C12) gets its own row because it is an MCT by chain length yet is absorbed substantially like an LCT — and coconut oil, this basket's own MCT source, is roughly half lauric. Publishing that against our own favourite fat is the point: a page that only disaggregates other people's food is marketing.

6 · THE ARBITRAGE — what the measurements say people actually miss

THE BRAKE, and it ships with the number every time. "Below the EAR" is a statistic about dietary intake, not a deficiency rate. Measured by serum 25(OH)D, about 5% of people are at risk of vitamin D deficiency against 96% below the dietary EAR — because the board set that RDA "on the assumption that people receive minimal sun exposure." Vitamin E intake is under-captured because cooking fats go uncounted, and choline has no EAR at all. Publishing 96 / 88 / 74% without this paragraph would be technically accurate and substantively misleading, which is the failure mode this page exists to refuse.
And the half that never gets quoted: selenium, niacin, riboflavin, phosphorus, iron (6%), thiamin, B12 and protein are not population shortfalls. A page that shows only the alarming half is a campaign, not a measurement.

7 · THE QUEST — a plan for a day, a week, a month, a year

Each rung is a real span of time and a different strength of evidence. You cannot claim a year-scale result from a day of remembering what you ate, so the rungs do not let you. Every quest is built and checked in Beehive University.

…AND HOW ANY OF IT COULD BE BELIEVED

Four different questions hide inside "how do we know it worked", and they have four different answers. Collapsing them is how wellness claims get made.
the questionthe honest answer herewho can check it
documented The plan is a file you compose and hold. This page has no account, no server and no database — it computes in your browser and stores nothing. The hive never receives your food log. You, completely. There is no custodian to trust, because there is no custodian.
monitored By nobody, by design. An operator who watches your intake is a party who can be subpoenaed, breached or sold. Autonomy means every factor is self-verifying — so monitoring is something you do to your own record, not something done to you. You. This is a deliberate capability we refuse to build, not one we have not built yet.
measured Two things get measured and they are not the same thing: what the food contains (this page, from published composition), and what your body did with it (a blood panel). Only the second is a measurement of you. Anyone, for the first. An accredited lab under chain of custody, for the second.
independently
verified		 This is where most of this page is genuinely strong, and where self-report is genuinely worthless. Every requirement traces to a published NASEM value; every food figure to a USDA record ID you can refetch; the EER equation is printed in full. A stranger can recompute this entire page from primary sources and catch us if we are wrong. What no stranger can verify is that you actually ate it. Anyone with a browser, for the arithmetic. Nobody, for adherence — unless you choose to attach evidence, which is what the ladder below is for.
THE EVIDENCE LADDER — every claim carries the tier it was earned at, and never a higher one.
tierwhat it evidenceswhat it cannot support
0 · composeda plan exists and its arithmetic is correct anything at all about a body. A plan is a hypothesis.
1 · self-reported your own account of what you ate the weakest tier in nutrition science and it is not close. Dietary self-report is systematically under-reported, and the error is not random — it varies with body weight, which biases exactly the comparison a page like this invites. Never present a tier-1 result as a finding.
2 · receipt-backed that the food entered your household — purchase records, a scale reading, a lot number on a bag that you ingested it, or when. It closes the honesty gap on supply, not on intake.
3 · assayed what is measurably in your blood — serum 25(OH)D, ferritin, B12, a lipid panel, omega-3 index. The only tier that measures the body rather than the food. attribution. A changed biomarker does not name its cause; that needs a design, a control and a pre-registered question — which is the BiGen lane, off this page.
The one number on this page that already sits at tier 3 for everyone: the vitamin D brake in section 6. Serum measurement says roughly 5% are deficient where dietary intake says 96% fall short. That gap between what people eat and what is in their blood is the reason the ladder exists, and it is why this project will not publish an intake statistic as a health finding.
