    WHY A DOCK, NOT A LOCALE LIST

    UNESCO: no less than 40% of some 6,700 languages are in danger of disappearing (unesco.org); the Atlas's last edition counted 538 critically + 502 severely + 632 definitely endangered + 607 vulnerable (Atlas record). a locale list adds languages when a market justifies them. a dock lets a language board because it exists.

    THE CONSENT LAYER — measurement, never decoration

    the sibling of BiGen's integrity layer, with the same symmetry law: consent blocks apply to English, to our own hub strings, to a fluent elder and to a machine draft — identically. the failure mode this layer exists to prevent is appropriation theater: a warm veneer over heritage extraction. anchored in CARE — Carroll et al. 2020, Data Science Journal (Collective benefit · Authority to control · Responsibility · Ethics, GIDA) and UNDRIP Articles 13–14. where CARE and permissionless docking tension, this surface shows the tension; it does not resolve it silently.
    consent:
  contributor:         git identity | persona nullifier — never the bzDiD root
                       (DisclosureMode per binding — BRIEF-04 §1, the attestation seam)
  community_attested:  true | false | not_sought   # a speaker/community of the language vouches
  provenance:          how the text came to be
  withdrawal:
    status:            none | withdrawn            # any contributor may withdraw THEIR OWN — permissionless
    effective:         YYYY-MM-DD                 # no one may remove another's, or the history
    history:           preserved, chain-anchored   # the dock forgets nothing; it honors withdrawals
  machine_generated:   true | false                # if true, renders LABELED — never silently
    three negative controls: the consent block rendering softer on our own material → fail · a withdrawn string still rendering → fail · a withdrawal erasing history → also fail · a machine-drafted string rendering unlabeled → fail. (machine is never silent about being machine — the unmeasured-never-zero law, translated.)

    THE STRING CORPUS — the hub's own strings, and the honest empty cells

    the corpus genesis: the dock starts by eating the organism's own UI. three lawful states per cell: community-attested (none yet), ⚙ machine-draft labeled (the father-tongue bridge, ruled 2026-08-20 — BLAI drafts through the pivot and the label never comes off until a community attests), or absent (never silently filled, never a silent English fallback pretending to be translation). the father column renders Русский; the genesis mother tongue latviešu enters through its pivots — the bridge's first live span, per FATHER-SET-1.
    a string absent in a language renders absent on every surface, labeled — never a silent fallback to English pretending to be the reader's language. a string record never displays dock-entry furniture (vitality badges): a category error the brief forbids.

    THE WORD ENTRY — skaists · Latvian · adjective

    One of our six doors is called skaists.social. It is a real Latvian word, and it means beautiful. This is its dictionary entry, laid out the way a dictionary lays one out — where the word came from, what it means (it means four things), and how it changes shape in a sentence.
    skaists — Latvian for beautiful. it started out meaning hot. hot → flushed → bright → beautiful. a word that walked from a fever to a face.
    Entry shape follows en.wiktionary.org: language as the heading, not the interface; etymology, IPA, numbered senses, inflection, cognates, source. Every string below is quoted from the cited source. The IPA cell is deliberately empty — the source's transcription could not be read cleanly through this seat's fetch, and this page does not print a phonetic string it cannot vouch for (SOURCES: cite or the cell stays silent).

    Etymology — quoted, Wiktionary after Karulis (1992, 2001)

    “This word results from the merging of two different lines: (a) a derivation from the same stem as skaidrs (“clear”): *skaid-tas > *skaista > skaists; the older “clear, pure” meaning can be found in examples up to the beginning of the 19th century; and (b) the past participle of kaist (“to be hot, to glow”), with an extra initial s from a former reflexive marker (compare apskaisties (“to get angry”) from ap-s-kaist-ies). The meaning probably changed as follows: “warm, hot, heated” > “red, flushed” > “bright, attractive, beautiful.” The older “red, flushed” meaning can be found in older sources and in folk tales (skaistiem vaigiem “with flushed cheeks”). Cognates include Lithuanian skaistùs, dialectal skáistas (“bright, light, clear, glittering, sparkling, beautiful, pure, chaste”).”

    Senses — four, numbered as the source numbers them

    1beautiful (having beauty; corresponding to certain aesthetic ideals, provoking certain aesthetic feelings)
    2beautiful, pleasant (which causes, gives pleasure)
    3beautiful, good (having good moral value, being socially useful)
    4(of animals, plants) beautiful, good (well kept, having desirable characteristics, e.g., for breeding)
    Sense 3 is the one the domain is named for: beautiful and good are the same word here — a thing is beautiful because it is socially useful. That is not a pun this estate invented; it is the third sense in the dictionary.
