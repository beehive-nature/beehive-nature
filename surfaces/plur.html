PEACELOVEUNITYRESPECT
a museum of the four words — every exhibit is either a cited RECORD or lore honestly labeled LORE
Welcome in. This is the story of four words that keep half a million strangers safe in a field for three days. Some of it is documented — those exhibits carry a green RECORD badge and a source you can click. Some of it lives the way culture actually lives, told and retold — those wear an amber LORE badge, and telling you which is which is how this museum respects you.
thirty years of floor wisdom, receipted. the word, the roots, the law they wrote against our beats, the handshake, the sensory floor, the living creed — six wings, every kernel labeled record or lore, nothing prettier than the truth. this is your house.
Method: a six-wing research fleet with an adversarial verify pass (every load-bearing claim re-fetched at its source; corrections are displayed in amber ⚖ lines, not silently patched). Primary archives where they exist — legislation.gov.uk enacted text, hyperreal.org via Wayback, municipal landmark records, court records as reported. A claim without a source does not enter; a telling with variants enters as LORE with its tellers named. The corpus law of this estate applied to cultural memory.

Ⅰ · WING I · THE WORD — where PLUR was born

The legend says one DJ shouted it into being. The archive says it took a train car, two coasts, a booklet, a mailing list, and a threat. Both are in this wing — labeled.
RECORD — cited
July 4, 1990 — the three words appear. Adam X — Frankie Bones' brother — paints "Peace Love Unity" on a train car. The earliest reported physical appearance of the slogan in the scene; the artifact itself does not survive. Bones himself calls 1990–1993 the "Peace Love Unity Movement" era — P.L.U.M., no R yet.
Vice, "How Frankie Bones' Storm Rave Birthed the PLUR Movement" (Dan Wender, 2015) — vice.com
RECORD — cited
Storm Rave, Brooklyn: May 11, 1991 → December 12, 1992. The party series that seeded the US rave movement ran about nineteen months and ended before the famous mic moment — a chronology that quietly undercuts every telling that places the fight "at a Storm Rave."
Vice (2015), dates as reported — vice.com
RECORD — cited
The R rode in from San Francisco. Around May 1993, Brian Behlendorf carries Geoff White's booklet Cybertribe Rising — with its essay on the "4 Pillars of the House Community," one pillar being respect — to the East Coast. At a renegade party in the RFK Stadium parking lot, Washington DC, early June 1993, when Laura LaGassa says "peace/love/unity," he adds:
"and don't forget Respect"
Laura LaGassa's eyewitness email to NE-Raves, Nov 18 1996, archived on hyperreal.org — Wayback copy (the live hyperreal path now 404s; the archive is the citable copy)
RECORD — cited
The essay went to a mailing list — not Usenet. LaGassa writes up the four pillars and posts to the NE-Raves mailing list (a University of Maryland listserv), early June 1993 — no acronym attached. The endlessly repeated "Respect was added on alt.rave" is not what the primary account says.
The essay survives: hyperreal.org via Wayback
verify pass: this card corrects this museum's own research brief, which had repeated the alt.rave version. The gate caught its own side first.
RECORD — cited
The acronym: a signature. Days later, ne-raver Rishad Quazi signs an email to the list "PLUR." Per LaGassa: "I'm about 99% sure it was Rishad. And if it wasn't Rishad, then it probably was Dan [Freelove]" — even the primary account carries its honest 1%.
"It caught on like wildfire"
LaGassa email, 1996, hyperreal.org via Wayback (link above)
RECORD — cited
The mic moment, eyewitness version. Late June 1993, a New York party thrown by the old Storm Rave crew during the New Music Seminar. A scuffle between rival gang members. Bones jumps on the turntables:
"…show some peace, love and unity right now I'm going to break your faces"
No "respect" in the shouted line — and the acronym was already circulating on the list before the shout. The legend runs the causality backwards.
LaGassa (she spun at this party), 1996 email, hyperreal.org via Wayback
RECORD — cited
Bones' own telling differs — and that is part of the record. His 2015 essay "Peace Love Unity Revisited" places the break-your-faces night in the Bronx, frames Respect as "the fourth pillar" born of that night's disrespect, and says his June 1993 record already carried "respect" on the label. That pressing, if verified against a physical copy, would be an independently datable artifact — this museum has not verified it, and says so.
Frankie Bones, Tumblr, "PEACE LOVE UNITY REVISITED" — tumblr.com/frankiebones · a record OF his telling, not confirmation of its contents
LORE — told as lore
The canonical legend, earliest archived telling. October 9, 1995: "stevie t," editor of Peace Love Rave Magazine, tells NE-Raves the old-skool story — Bones giving a visionary speech at a Storm Rave, the whole warehouse waving hands in unity, the speech retold until it became PLUR. Beautiful, load-bearing, and told here as what it is: the tribe's own founding myth, already polished by 1995.
stevie t via NE-Raves, archived on hyperreal.org's PLUR Origins page (Wayback link above)
RECORD — cited
The kernel this estate keeps: the man who appended the R — Brian Behlendorf — went on to co-create the Apache web server, and hyperreal.org, the archive that preserves PLUR's primary record, is his project. The fourth pillar of the rave and the open web share a hand. Respect, it turns out, ships as infrastructure.

Ⅱ · WING II · THE INHERITANCE — the floor was Black, brown, and queer first

PLUR did not invent the sanctuary floor. It inherited one — built decades earlier by people the outside world would not have. This wing is the deed of inheritance.
RECORD — cited
Valentine's Day 1970 — "Love Saves the Day." David Mancuso's first Loft party, 647 Broadway, NYC: invitation-only, nothing sold, running past legal hours. The invitation's initials wink at LSD while announcing an ethic of universal love.
"Once you walked into the Loft you were cut off from the outside world. You got into a timeless, mindless state."
Mancuso, quoted in Tim Lawrence, Love Saves the Day (Duke Univ. Press, 2003) — timlawrence.info
RECORD — cited
Privacy was the legal architecture of the sanctuary. Because nothing was sold, the Loft sat outside licensing law — a design consciously drawing on the Harlem rent-party tradition of the 1920s. Mancuso described his own mixed crowd as "gay, straight, white, Spanish" — his words. Larry Levan, Frankie Knuckles, and Nicky Siano were shaped on this floor.
Lawrence (2003); the crowd description is Mancuso's own interview phrasing
RECORD — cited
Paradise Garage, 84 King Street: Jan 28, 1978 → Sep 26, 1987. Members-only, no alcohol, Larry Levan resident — and per the NYC LGBT Historic Sites Project, it "did not discriminate among its membership based on race, class, or sexual identity." Its model was the Loft's, carried forward — the institutional bridge from 1970 to everything rave inherited.
NYC LGBT Historic Sites Project; lineage per Brewster & Broughton, Last Night a DJ Saved My Life (1999)
RECORD — cited
The Warehouse, 206 S. Jefferson St, Chicago — 1977, Frankie Knuckles resident to 1982. A private membership club, Saturday midnight to Sunday noon. Knuckles, 1995, on who the floor sheltered:
"predominantly black, predominantly gay… Very soulful and very spiritual." … "For most of the people that went there, it was church for them."
Frankie Knuckles 1995 interview, republished by Red Bull Music Academy Daily (2018); building facts per the City of Chicago Landmarks designation report
verify note: the more famous variant — "church for people who have fallen from grace" — is quoted everywhere, but its original interview could not be traced. The verifiable quote is displayed; the famous one is named as unverified.
LORE — told as lore
How "house" got its name — two tellings, side by side. The bin-label telling: Importes Etc. on Printer's Row labeled records "As Heard at The Warehouse," shortened to "House" (told by Chip E. and store veterans; variants exist). And Knuckles' own telling, on the record in 1995: a South Side tavern sign reading "WE PLAY HOUSE MUSIC" — "It's the same stuff that you play at The Warehouse." The museum displays both; the culture never picked one.
Importes Etc. oral history; Knuckles 1995 interview (RBMA Daily, 2018)
RECORD — cited
June 2023: the City of Chicago designates 206 S. Jefferson a Chicago Landmark as the birthplace of house music. Official municipal recognition that a Black gay membership club is the origin point of the genre — dedication block party, October 5, 2024.
City of Chicago, Dept. of Planning & Development landmark designation (2023)
RECORD — cited
Detroit: the Belleville Three. Juan Atkins, Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson — high-school friends — create techno; the 1988 UK compilation Techno! The New Dance Sound of Detroit fixes the name in public. May, 1988:
"The music is just like Detroit, a complete mistake. It's like George Clinton and Kraftwerk are stuck in an elevator with only a sequencer to keep them company."
Stuart Cosgrove, "Seventh City Techno," The Face, May 1988; compilation per Discogs. (Sources split on whether the quote first ran in The Face or the liner notes — both are 1988 print.)

⚓ THE ISLAND · EIVISSA — for much of the world, rave was born here

For much of the world, rave was born here. Not the music — Chicago's Warehouse and Detroit's Belleville Three made that, and Wing II holds their deed. The wave was born here: a permissive window the transition opened, an uncovered dancefloor under the trees, a mayor who blessed the party from the stage, and four London DJs who went home and lit 1988. Around that showcase, the island's own twenty-six centuries — a dancing god on the money, a goddess in the cave, a philosopher on the beach. The showcase first; the deep history after, as it always was.
RECORD — cited
THE BIRTHPLACE — the claim, on the record. The genre at the root of UK acid house is literally named for these islands — Balearic beat, for the Balearics — and its canonical author is Amnesia's resident DJ Alfredo, whom the dance press titles the "Father of the Balearic beat." In summer 1987 four London DJs heard him at Amnesia and went home to open Shoom, Spectrum and The Trip; 1988 became the Second Summer of Love. That is why the dance press treats Ibiza as the spiritual home of rave for much of the world — an export chain with the island at its source, held honestly below in its record and lore halves.
"Balearic beat was named for its popularity among European nightclub and beach rave patrons on the Balearic island of Ibiza, a popular tourist destination."
Wikipedia, "Balearic beat" (quote verbatim) · DJ Mag obituary, "DJ Alfredo, Ibiza legend and 'Father of the Balearic beat'" · the 1987 cards below carry the chain in full
RECORD — cited
THE SPANISH DOOR — the crown quote, verified to the night. Madrid, 28–29 January 1984: the Fiesta del Estudiante y la Radio — "24 Horas de Música y Radio," organized by RNE Radio 3's Tiempo de Universidad — fills the Palacio de los Deportes. Mayor Enrique Tierno Galván, from the stage, to the rockeros:
"Rockeros: el que no esté colocado, que se coloque y al loro"
Colocado: slang for high. Al loro: Madrid slang for keep your eyes open. An elected mayor blessing the party from an official stage, to applause — nine years after Franco (dead 20 November 1975, ending nearly four decades of dictatorship, into a peaceful transition to democracy). The single best exhibit of official tolerance this wing holds. Ten years later the British Parliament answered the same music by defining its beat in statute to ban it — Wing III holds that door's other side.
El País, 29 January 1984 (contemporaneous report; quote verbatim including the opening "Rockeros:") · Archivo RTVE footage, "el concierto del Estudiante y la Radio de 1984" · Franco dates per Britannica
verify pass: several retellings misplace the line at the San Isidro fiestas or a spring festival — the contemporaneous El País report and the RTVE archive fix it at the January 1984 student-radio festival. The "III Gran" edition number and the Santo Tomás de Aquino framing appear in neither source and are dropped.
RECORD — cited
The door's legal hinge — decriminalization was a pre-existing condition, not a reform. Spain never criminalized drug possession for personal use: a 1974 Supreme Court doctrine held possession criminal only when intended for third-party consumption —
"In 1974, the Spanish Supreme Court clarified that possession would only be criminal if intended for third-party consumption, exempting personal use from prosecution—a ruling maintained to this day."
The 1983 penal reform narrowed punishable conduct further and wrote the distinction between seriously harmful substances and cannabis into statute-era practice. The window closed in 1992: the Ley Corcuera (LO 1/1992) made public consumption a grave administrative infraction — fined, never a crime — while private use stayed outside the criminal law entirely, BOE art. 25.1 verbatim:
"el consumo en lugares, vías, establecimientos o transportes públicos, así como la tenencia ilícita, aunque no estuviera destinada al tráfico, de drogas tóxicas, estupefacientes o sustancias psicotrópicas"
The permissive window c. 1975–1992 is exactly when the island's open-air scene formed.
TalkingDrugs, "Spain's Decriminalisation: Decades of Quiet Innovation" (Valldaura, Ojeda, Rosell & Roig, 2025 — quote verbatim) · BOE-A-1992-4252, art. 25.1 (verbatim) · 1983 reform per OJP/NCJRS abstract + TNI Spain country report
RECORD — cited
The material fact: the floor had no roof. Through the 1980s the flagship clubs ran open-air and all-night with no equivalent of UK licensing hours — DJ Alfredo began his Amnesia residency in 1983 on an uncovered dancefloor under the trees, sets running to sunrise, while Ku drew crowds in the thousands around its open-air pool:
"He was given his first Amnesia gig in 1983 and played under the trees that still stand, on the open-air dancefloor that is now covered."
This is what visiting Britons had no legal way to reproduce at home — the climate made the architecture. And the 1990s closed both windows at once: national law in 1992, and stricter noise and licensing regulation that roofed the open-air floors — Amnesia complying with a retractable roof over the Terrace.
"The nineties brought stricter noise and licensing regulations to Ibiza. Amnesia acquiesced to municipal regulations with a new retractable roof over the Terrace."
DJ Mag, "Celebrating 50 years of Amnesia" (both quotes verbatim); Amnesia's own history page · the finca and its resident have their own cards directly below
RECORD — cited
THE MOLECULE — history only: dates, people, laws, one botanical family tree. No chemistry, no process, no dosage; that ceiling is this card's law. The paper trail begins as a clerical line: MDMA first appears in Merck's records in 1912, lab-journal name "Methylsafrylamin," an unnamed intermediate in German patent No. 274 350 (filed 24 December 1912) on a route to a styptic — a clotting agent. It was never the product. The famous origin — "Merck made it in 1912 as an appetite suppressant" — is a myth the company's own archive demolished: no appetite-suppressant program existed 1900–1960, and the substance was not pharmacologically tested at all until 1927, work then halted for economic reasons (the rumor's likely root: a confusion with MDA, which Smith Kline French did test in the 1950s).
"Merck applied for this patent to protect an alternative chemical method for synthesizing the styptic hydrastinine, not appetite suppressants."
Alexander Shulgin did not invent it — he reintroduced it: re-synthesized and self-tested September 1976, passed to psychotherapist Leo Zeff in 1977, and in 1978 published with David E. Nichols the first report of its human psychopharmacology — the paper trail behind his erroneous "father of MDMA" title. Then the quiet therapeutic era: an estimated few dozen therapists (1978–85), Zeff introducing it to about four thousand people and training more than 150 therapists, Greer and Tolbert treating over eighty patients 1980–85 — their published reports became classics of the era's clinical literature.
Bernschneider-Reif, Öxler & Freudenmann, Pharmazie 61 (2006) — the Merck archive study (quote verbatim) · Freudenmann et al., Addiction 101 (2006) · Passie, Drug Science, Policy and Law 4 (2018) · Benzenhöfer & Passie, Addiction 105 (2010)
verify pass: the therapy-era line ships corrected — Greer & Tolbert's reports are "classics in the field" (the source's own words), not "the era's main clinical account"; and the few-dozen estimate covers 1978–1985.
LORE — told as lore
The molecule's street lore — the name, the open bar, the island leg. "Ecstasy" dates to circa 1981, chosen by a Los Angeles distribution network purely as marketing — the anonymous distributor, quoted in Bruce Eisner's Ecstasy: The MDMA Story (1989):
"it would sell better than calling it 'Empathy'"
Dallas, the Starck Club (opened May 1984): while it was still legal, ecstasy was sold openly across the bar — by bartenders and via the cigarette girl — a trade the club's oral history attributes to individual employees, not management. And how it reached Ibiza in the mid-1980s: the standard histories point to the Bhagwan Rajneesh sannyasin network, which Matthew Collin's Altered State documents building distribution channels beyond the US — but the specific island-arrival story remains anecdotal; David Hillier, checking it, found no smoking gun. Three tellings, tellers named; that is what lore is.
Eisner (1989) via hedweb.com · Red Bull Music Academy Daily, "Nightclubbing: Starck Club" (2016 — named tellers) · Collin, Altered State (1997) tested in Vice (2018)
RECORD — cited
The scheduling, the family tree, the other documented life. Texas: Michael Clegg's "Texas Group" mass-distributed the tablet in Dallas before the ban — the DEA's own figure, as reported: "distributing upwards of 500,000 pills a month in Dallas at the time." The answer was the DEA's first-ever use of its emergency scheduling power — Federal Register 31 May 1985, effective 1 July 1985:
"EFFECTIVE DATE: On July 1, 1985, MDMA will be subject to Schedule I control."
The botanical ancestry, stated as encyclopedic fact and nothing more: safrole — the principal component of sassafras oil, from the sassafras tree (Sassafras albidum) — is the classic historically documented precursor family associated with MDMA, and is internationally controlled as a listed precursor:
"Safrole is listed as a Table I precursor under the United Nations Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances."
The founder's "sassafras tree bark oil" is exactly this family tree — named as ancestry, never conflating the tree with the synthetic. And the molecule's other documented life continues: MAPS's phase-3 trials met their primary endpoints (MAPP1, Nature Medicine 2021; MAPP2, 2023), but on 4 June 2024 an FDA advisory committee voted against approval and on 9 August 2024 the FDA issued Lykos Therapeutics a Complete Response Letter requiring another phase-3 trial — published by the FDA itself 4 September 2025. Unapproved as of 2026; the research arc goes on.
RBMA Daily / Texas Monthly (DEA figure quote verbatim) · Federal Register 50 FR 23118 (quote verbatim, public domain) · Wikipedia "Safrole" (UN 1988 Table I, quote verbatim) · Lykos press release + MAPS statement on the CRL

CANNABIS & THE KINGDOM — the legal ground. Personal consumption is not criminalized in Spain and carries an administrative penalty only in public; beneath it sits the Supreme Court's consumo compartido doctrine — closed-group, non-commercial consumption is not inherently criminal — the legal seed of the cannabis club model. The honest counterweight, in force 1 July 2015: the Ley Mordaza (LO 4/2015) makes public consumption or possession a serious administrative infraction under art. 36.16, fined €601–30,000 — no criminal record, a real penalty, heavily used. Private use remains outside both criminal and administrative sanction.
"Drug consumption and possession for personal use are not criminalized in Spain and carry an administrative penalty only when performed in a public place"
ICEERS, Spain legal information (quote verbatim) · BOE, LO 4/2015 arts. 36.16 + 39.1 (fine band verified in the consolidated text)
verify pass: a popular framing attributes the doctrine to the 1978 Constitution's privacy protections — the cited source says no such thing, and the attribution is dropped here. The source itself flags the doctrine as "recently revised": the 2015 Supreme Court ceiling, two cards down.
RECORD — cited
The clubs — legalisation without commercialisation. The Basque origin: Kalamudia's 1997 public protest plantation, repeated without prosecution, dissolved around 2001–02 into local associations cultivating for their own members. Pannagh: founded in Bilbao 22 February 2003, registered 20 June 2003; its president Martín Barriuso arrested with two members while harvesting for the club in 2005 — and the favorable Basque court outcome in 2006 (confiscated cannabis returned) is credited with precipitating the nationwide expansion. The model as its international students codify it:
"Cannabis social clubs (CSCs) are private, non-profit organisations in which cannabis is collectively grown and distributed to registered members."
The first CSC dates to 2001; roughly 400 clubs exist; Uruguay later wrote CSCs into statute; "the growth of the CSC model in Spain demonstrates that cannabis legalisation does not have to mean commercialisation." The boom's numbers: Catalonia went from about 40 clubs in 2011 to more than 500 with over 165,000 members by 2014 (a club-federation president's count — an advocate's figures, flagged as such) — the press dubbing Barcelona "the Amsterdam of southern Europe." The arc bends back: the Constitutional Court annulled Navarra's foral regulation (STC 144/2017) and Catalonia's Law 13/2017 in full (STC 100/2018, 19 September 2018) — narcotics regulation is exclusive state competence:
"marijuana's regulation must be in the hands of the State, which has sole responsibility for establishing the criminal penalties for narcotics"
And the Supreme Court's 2015 Ebers line drew the criminal ceiling: organized cultivation and distribution by a club of 290 members constitutes trafficking — large open-membership clubs over the line, the small closed-circle doctrine intact beneath it.
Pannagh's own history + ENCOD (dates confirmed) · Transform Drug Policy Foundation briefing (quotes verbatim) · Reason (2016 — boom figures) · Ara English (STC quotes) · Poder Judicial news release (Ebers, STS 484/2015)
verify pass: the Barcelona enforcement line ships corrected — over 50 clubs closed under Mayor Trias's threatened 150-meter zoning plan (which would have shut about 80%), reversed by Mayor Colau before it took effect; the widely repeated "a third of the city's 145 clubs ordered closed" is not in the cited source. Malta is likewise dropped — the briefing names only Uruguay.
RECORD — cited
Hemp — the kingdom's strategic fiber. From 1665 to 1808 the Spanish crown ran a sustained military supply system for cáñamo: hemp made the fleet's match-cord, rigging and sails, and royal policy tried to extend its harvest to the American possessions to escape dependence on Russian hemp bought in the Baltic:
"con él se producía cuerda mecha, y jarcia y velas para el aparejo de los veleros"
Callosa de Segura (Alicante) is the living monument — self-styled capital del cáñamo, Spain's leading maker of hemp rope, nets and espadrille soles until synthetic fibers displaced the craft in the 1960s; its Museo del Cáñamo (founded 1986, an official Museographic Collection since 24 March 1994) exhibits the whole cycle from seed to spun thread. (The popular telling dates the town's hemp working to c. 1300 and calls medieval Callosa the largest producer — trade lore, carried as tradition, not documented.) Modern lawful hemp: under the EU framework only certified-seed varieties below 0.3% THC qualify, and the EU's fibre-hemp area grew from 20,540 ha (2015) to 33,020 ha (2022) — France, Germany and the Netherlands lead; Spain cultivates inside the framework, a legal fact, not a leadership claim. This estate keeps its own hemp line on the board: the hemp-hearts commons price feed on the DAO dashboard.
Díaz-Ordóñez, Espacio Tiempo y Forma IV/32 (2019) — crown supply system (quote verbatim, ES) · Ayuntamiento de Callosa de Segura + es.wikipedia (museum dates) · European Commission DG AGRI hemp page (quotes verbatim)
RECORD — cited
The island inside the model. Ibiza's scene runs on the same national theory — and it is municipally documented, not just promoted: the city council has issued opening licenses to 17 cannabis associations since 2018, and in March 2024 voted to regulate new openings through a municipal ordinance, suspending licenses for new associations meanwhile:
"el Consistorio ha dado licencia de apertura a 17 de estos establecimientos desde 2018"
November 2023: members of the island's Domus Club protested outside the Ibiza courts over a police raid they said was carried out without a judicial order. The argument the kingdom has had for thirty years, the island has out loud too.
Periódico de Ibiza (26 March 2024 — quote verbatim) · Cañamo magazine (Domus Club raid coverage)
RECORD — cited
Amnesia: a farmhouse renamed by a philosopher. A centuries-old finca near Sant Rafel — five generations of the Planells family — sold in 1970, then leased in May 1976 by Madrid philosopher Antonio Escohotado (later the author of Historia general de las drogas), who wanted a name that meant going out to forget your problems. It opened for dancing in 1976:
"non-stop dancing around the small central water pool"
— and through the 1980s the floor had no roof. The open sky is the physical precondition of the Balearic sunrise set.
DJ Mag, "Celebrating 50 years of Amnesia" anniversary feature
RECORD — cited
Alfredo — the exile who built the floor. Alfredo Fiorito: born Rosario, Argentina, 1953; a journalist. He left in 1976, after the coup — his own words:
"I leave my country for political reasons."
— invoking the Proceso and the 30,000 disappeared. He reached Ibiza that September; candlemaker, delivery driver, bartender, then the decks. First Amnesia gig 1983; resident from 1984; the all-genres sunrise style — house beside disco, reggae, flamenco, Pink Floyd — that the UK would later bottle as "Balearic": "I blurred the division between black and white music." His own memory of the floor: "There were Italians, french, four or three English, Spanish, South Americans, Germans" — and his record box built to serve all of them at once. He died on the island December 24, 2024, aged 71. Amnesia's farewell: "Your music and vision shaped the sound of Balearic beat."
Test Pressing, "DJ Alfredo — R.I.P. In His Own Words"; RA / DJ Mag / Mixmag obituaries (death date verified across all three)
verify note: the widely quoted "straight, gay, bi-sexual, young, old…" crowd description traces to Space Ibiza's promotional bio, not to verified Alfredo speech — his verbatim floor description above is the one on the record. The mixed-floor ethos itself is the veteran's own telling: memoir, honored as such.
RECORD — cited
The 1987 pipeline — record half. In summer 1987, around Paul Oakenfold's 24th birthday, Oakenfold, Danny Rampling, Nicky Holloway and Johnny Walker heard Alfredo at Amnesia — a trip entwined, in their own accounts, with first ecstasy. The trip is documented: "it's certainly true to say that the trip happened." Home again: Rampling launches Shoom (late 1987 — Wing III picks up this thread), Oakenfold opens Spectrum (April 1988, after his smaller Future night), Holloway launches The Trip (May 1988). Walker founded no club — he played Oakenfold's nights; the legend usually forgets that.

LORE — told as lore
The 1987 pipeline — lore half. "Four DJs brought house music to Britain" is the participant-told founding myth, polished across decades of their own retellings. House was already in the UK — Jazzy M's pirate show from 1986, house at the Haçienda's Nude night from 1986 — and UK–Ibiza traffic predates the trip (Trevor Fung had DJed the island since 1982). The pipeline is real; the origin-point framing is canon, not chronicle. Alfredo's own wry postscript, on the record:
"When I first went to play in London I discovered that everyone was playing my records."
Oakenfold/Rampling/Holloway oral histories; the challenge documented by Ears To The House; Alfredo per Test Pressing
RECORD — cited
The oldest floor: Sa Caleta, late 8th century BC. A Phoenician settlement on the south coast — stone buildings, communal ovens, metalworking — abandoned around 600 BC in a planned, peaceful move to the bay that became the town of Ebusus. The "traditional" founding date of 654 BC comes from Diodorus Siculus: "The date of the founding of the colony falls one hundred and sixty years after the settlement of Carthage."
Sa Caleta site museum (Consell d'Eivissa); Diodorus Siculus, Library of History 5.16 (Loeb, at LacusCurtius)
verify pass: 654 BC is a modern computation — Diodorus gives only "160 years after Carthage"; the 814 BC anchor is Timaeus's date, supplied in the Loeb editor's footnote. Archaeology at Sa Caleta predates the literary date by roughly two generations.
RECORD — cited
The centerpiece: the name itself. Ibiza/Eivissa descends from the Phoenician-Punic ʾYBŠM (Iboshim) via Greek Ebousos and Latin Ebusus — and the favored scholarly reading (J. M. Solà-Solé) is "island of Bes." Bes: the Egyptian dwarf god of music, dance, and the protection of households — warder of snakes, adorning doorways, beloved without a single temple. The material evidence is the island's own money: Punic coins minted on Ebusus from the 3rd century BC pair the name with the image of Bes — the dominant coin type for centuries, imitated as far as Pompeii. In Solà-Solé's telling, settlers credited the island's famed lack of venomous animals to Bes's snake-warding and named the island for him. An honest label says "commonly interpreted as," never "means" — an alternative reads the name as "island of fragrance." But the coins say what they say: an island that stamped the god of the dance on its money. This estate, whose every door begins with a b, takes note.
Ashmolean Museum, "Bes: Ancient Egyptian God, Fighter, Dancer, Companion"; Ibicasa island-history series (Solà-Solé attribution); numismatic record via the Ebusus/Bes coin catalogues
RECORD — cited
The goddess in the cave. Es Culleram, discovered 1907: a Punic sanctuary of Tanit in Ibiza's northeast — on the order of 600 whole terracotta figures and over a thousand more heads, many the winged-shawled bust of the goddess; a double-faced plaque (found 1917) carries an older dedication to Resef-Melkart and a later one to Tanit — the cult changing hands in stone. Below the town, Puig des Molins: the largest and best-preserved Phoenician-Punic necropolis in the western Mediterranean (grave counts vary by survey — thousands of hypogea; UNESCO's data says 5,000+ graves over 5+ hectares).
ibiza.travel heritage pages (Consell d'Eivissa); MAEF — Museu Arqueològic d'Eivissa i Formentera; UNESCO property 417 data
LORE — told as lore
Es Vedrà — the island's offshore mythology, sorted honestly. The sirens who tempted Odysseus lived there, says the telling — no ancient source places them anywhere near; the attribution is modern island tradition. "The third most magnetic point on Earth," says the other telling — no study exists, and the islet is limestone with no iron; its own tellers say "it is believed," never citing a measurement. And Tanit's holy island, says the third — the living mythology projecting the island's genuine, excavated Tanit cult onto the rock offshore. Three tellings, all real as tellings; the receipts live in the cave and the necropolis.
Island tradition per Diario de Ibiza and travel literature; geology per Emily Kaufman, "The History Buff's Guide to Ibiza"
RECORD — cited
UNESCO, 1999: "Ibiza, Biodiversity and Culture." A mixed cultural-natural listing: Dalt Vila's Renaissance fortifications, Sa Caleta, Puig des Molins — and
"the best-conserved marine meadows of Posidonia Oceanica (Neptune Grass) in the Mediterranean"
The famous "100,000-year-old" Posidonia claim is published science with honest error bars: a 2012 PLoS ONE study found a single clone spanning ~15 km near Formentera, its age model-dependent on assumed growth rates. Old beyond argument; the headline number is a model.
UNESCO World Heritage Centre, property 417 (quote verbatim); Arnaud-Haond et al., PLoS ONE 7(2): e30454 (2012)
RECORD — cited
1932: the philosopher on the beach. Walter Benjamin lived and wrote on Ibiza twice — April–July 1932, and again in 1933 fleeing Hitler's Germany — based around Sant Antoni, sending frequent letters including to Gershom Scholem. In an April 1932 letter he already frets that a hotel being built at the port will change the island's character. The first documented case of the island's oldest ritual: arriving, and worrying about who arrives next.
Vicente Valero, Experiencia y pobreza: Walter Benjamin en Ibiza (2001/2017); Cartas de la época de Ibiza (2008)
RECORD — cited
The hippie loading dock — and 1973, the hinge year. American draft resisters avoiding Vietnam and European dropouts escaping post-war conformity settled in communes in the island's rural reaches. In 1973 the bohemia acquired institutions: the Hotel Club Punta Arabí invited the island's hippies to sell crafts to its guests — the Punta Arabí Hippy Market, still the island's first and largest — and Pacha opened in the Ses Feixes wetlands facing Ibiza Town, built like an Ibicencan farmhouse, six years after Ricardo Urgell's first Pacha in Sitges. Bar Anita in Sant Carles is remembered as the settlers' hub — the village's only telephone, the de facto post office — a telling preserved by everyone and archived by no one.
Diario de Ibiza; Illes Balears tourism board (market, 1973, verified); Wikipedia: The Pacha Group (verified); Bar Anita per local heritage tellings — that last card is lore-grade and labeled in-line
RECORD — cited
The sunset counter-gift: chillout. Café del Mar opens June 20, 1980 in Sant Antoni — a bar aimed at the sunset, interior by Lluís Güell. José Padilla, resident from 1991 after years of selling the venue his sunset mixtapes, launches the Café del Mar compilations in 1994 and selects the first six — the template the whole chillout genre was pressed from. He died October 18, 2020, aged 64; the venue's tribute: "the Godfather of Chillout." And in 1988 the UK bottled the uptempo side as a genre: Balearic Beats Vol. 1 (FFRR — Oakenfold, Tong, Fung; sleeve notes by Terry Farley) — a landmark widely regarded as codifying the Balearic sound for the UK market.
cafedelmar.com/about (verified); Wikipedia: José Padilla (DJ); RA obituary; Discogs FFRR 1988 + The Ransom Note

RECORD — cited
The superclub arc, with its receipts. Ku opens 1979 in San Rafael; renamed Privilege in 1995; the 2003 Guinness World Records lists it the world's largest nightclub — 6,500 m², capacity 10,000; closed after 2019; the site reopened as UNVRS in 2025. Space (1989, Pepe Roselló) ends its 27-year run October 2, 2016 with a ~20-hour closing fiesta, 100+ DJs, Carl Cox playing the final track. DC10, an unmarked farmhouse at the end of the airport runway, hosts the first Circoloco in July 1999 — a free Monday after-hours for roughly 80 people, no headliners, no ads — and grows into the underground's defining institution. (Sources disagree on DC10's founding year, 1989 vs 1990 — displayed, not resolved.)
Wikipedia: Privilege Ibiza (Guinness 2003 via its citation) / UNVRS / DC10; Ibiza Spotlight review of the last Space fiesta
RECORD — cited
And the state-vs-dance wing echoes on the island itself. October 2017: San Antonio moves to declare the West End a Special Acoustic Protection Zone after summer noise hit 85.9 dB — 20 over the legal limit — imposing 65 dB day / 55 dB night. "This could and should be a turning point for the area." May 2024: shop alcohol sales banned 9:30pm–8am in the party zones under the Balearic decree against excesses. May 24, 2024: roughly 1,000 march with Prou Eivissa ("Enough Ibiza") for a tourism moratorium and housing for locals — tourism being over 80% of the island's economy. The island that taught the world the open floor now argues, out loud and in law, about who the floor is for. That argument is the tradition too.

Ⅲ · WING III · THE STATE vs THE DANCE — the law that named the music

In 1994 the United Kingdom wrote a genre of music into criminal statute. This wing holds the exact words — and what the culture did next.
RECORD — cited
The centerpiece. Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, section 63(1)(b), enacted text, verbatim:
"music" includes sounds wholly or predominantly characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats
Royal Assent November 3, 1994. The only place in UK statute law where a genre of music is effectively defined — written to empower police to shut down raves.
legislation.gov.uk — s.63, enacted · verified verbatim against the statute by this museum's fact-check pass
RECORD — cited
The threshold tightened later. As enacted, s.63 reached open-air gatherings of 100+ people where amplified music plays at night, "likely to cause serious distress to the inhabitants of the locality." The Anti-social Behaviour Act 2003 cut the number to 20.
legislation.gov.uk — enacted vs current text
verify pass correction: the cut to 20 applies to England & Wales; the Scottish version of s.63 retains the 100-person threshold.
RECORD — cited
What they were afraid of started in a gym. Shoom — Danny Rampling's weekly all-nighter in a Southwark Street fitness centre, late 1987, straight off the Ibiza/Amnesia trip — ignites UK acid house; 1988–89 becomes the Second Summer of Love, smiley-face emblem and all. By 1989 the scene is throwing "orbital" raves around the M25 — Sunrise, Energy, Biology — with meeting points released through BT's Voicebank answerphones, tens of thousands strong.
Mixmag Summer of Love retrospectives; Red Bull "The road to rave" (M25); Wikipedia: Shoom / Second Summer of Love / Acid house party
LORE — told as lore
Whose smiley? The best-known telling puts the acid-house smiley on Danny Rampling's Shoom flyers from early 1988; another credits the Bomb the Bass "Beat Dis" sleeve (February 1988). Both artifacts are real and dated; who was first genuinely varies by teller.
Scene oral history, retold in Matthew Collin's Altered State and elsewhere
RECORD — cited
Castlemorton Common, May 22–29, 1992. The UK's largest illegal rave — estimates 20,000 to 40,000 — after police diverted traveller convoys from the Avon Free Festival. The media storm around it is consistently cited as the direct inspiration for the 1994 Act's rave provisions. Thirteen members of Spiral Tribe were tried for it at Worcester Crown Court in one of the longest, most expensive cases in British legal history — around £4 million. All were acquitted.
DJ Mag, "The history of Spiral Tribe"; Wikipedia: Castlemorton Common Festival (attendance varies by source)
LORE — told as lore
The travellers' telling: the police made the festival. Operation Nomad shunted the convoys over the county line, West Mercia stood back, and the first-night TV bulletins were the best advertisement a free party ever had. The diversion and the coverage are documented; "they let it happen" is a framing told differently by ravers, residents, and both police forces.
Free-party oral tradition, retold in DJ Mag, Vice, and BBC retrospectives
RECORD — cited
The culture answered in three registers. On the street: the Advance Party alliance and three London "Kill the Bill" demonstrations in 1994, ending in Hyde Park on October 9. On wax: Autechre's Anti EP (Warp, Sept 1994), whose sealed sticker warned two tracks contained repetitive beats — while the third was engineered so that
"Flutter has been programmed in such a way that no bars contain identical beats"
— protest written in the drum programming itself. And on the road: Spiral Tribe and the sound systems took their rigs to Europe, seeding the teknival movement. The law passed; the dance routed around it.
GOV.UK FOI release on the 1994 CJB protests; Anti EP sticker text per Discogs/Warp documentation; DJ Mag Spiral Tribe history

Ⅳ · WING IV · THE RITUAL — kandi, the handshake, respect in practice

A creed you can shake hands with. Four gestures, a bracelet, free water, and the strange history of governments deciding whether care is contraband.
1 · PEACE
2 · LOVE
3 · UNITY
4 · RESPECT
RECORD — cited
The handshake, as practiced: peace signs touch fingertip-to-fingertip; two half-hearts join; hands clasp, fingers interlocked; then —
"trade your kandi through your interlocked hands, one at a time"
Etiquette rides with it: tradeables on the right arm, keepsakes on the left; never ask for a specific piece; never re-trade kandi made for you.
The ritual, live: 🍬 the kandi bar — compose one, do the four-step handshake, gift it; the etiquette above enforced in code.
iHeartRaves, "How to Trade Kandi at a Rave" — the ritual as codified in-scene
verify pass: the popular "bracelet slid over still-clasped hands" phrasing is not what the source says — the trade goes through the interlocked hands. Corrected on display.
LORE — told as lore
Where the handshake and kandi come from — honestly, nobody has the receipt. No dated primary source fixes the four-step exchange's origin; every credible telling makes it a 2000s codification, learned person-to-person (the USC Digital Folklore Archives collects it from raver informants). Kandi itself is traced in community tellings to 1990s "candy ravers" and the friendship-bracelet exchange. The gestures postdate the 1993 motto by about a decade.
USC Digital Folklore Archives, "PLUR Handshake and the Exchanging of Kandi"; scene guides (Freedom Rave Wear, iHeartRaves)
RECORD — cited
Respect-in-practice was raver-built — and it predates the famous names. Toronto's TRIP! (est. 1995): "born … out of a need for appropriate drug and sex information within the Toronto rave scene." Then DanceSafe, founded 1998 by Emanuel Sferios in the SF Bay Area — public drug-checking and volunteer booths stocked with
"free condoms, earplugs, water, fact-based drug information, and consent education materials"
dancesafe.org; tripproject.ca · both founding facts verified
RECORD — cited
PLURR — the fifth letter. A circulating variant adds a second R: Responsibility — to self and community, foregrounding harm reduction. The variant's existence in print is record; every single-origin story for it is lore, credited only to unnamed veteran ravers.
Wikipedia "PLUR" (documents the variant); scene guides
RECORD — cited
2001, New Orleans: care prosecuted as contraband. Federal prosecutors used the "crack house" statute against the State Palace Theatre rave promoters — citing bottled water, an air-conditioned chill room, glow sticks, pacifiers and massage tables as evidence of facilitating drug use. The ACLU later got the glowstick/pacifier ban lifted on First Amendment grounds. The exact items this wing calls respect-in-practice were, for a while, evidence.
Alternet, "Chemical Warfare: The RAVE Act" (2002); ACLU statements of the period
RECORD — cited
The UK wrote the opposite answer. Home Office "Safer Clubbing" guidance (2002):
"It is therefore imperative that there is free and unrestricted, but monitored, access to cold drinking water at all times."
By 2010/2014 free potable water on request was a mandatory statutory condition on all alcohol-licensed premises: "The responsible person must ensure that free potable water is provided on request…" The rave-era water fight, ended by statute — in the ravers' favor.
Safer Clubbing (Home Office/LDPF, 2002), para 2.20 verified verbatim; Licensing Act 2003 Mandatory Conditions Order 2014, legislation.gov.uk
RECORD — cited
Consent, formalized. The Association of Independent Festivals' "Safer Spaces" charter (2017, relaunched 2022, with Rape Crisis England & Wales, Good Night Out, Safe Gigs for Women, Girls Against, UN Women) defines consent as agreement "by choice … freedom and capacity to make that choice," revocable at any time, and teaches bystander intervention. Respect, operationalized — the fourth word grew teeth.
Association of Independent Festivals, Safer Spaces at Festivals charter

Ⅴ · WING V · THE SENSORY FLOOR — the dance floor as a neurodivergent sanctuary

The founder of this estate calls his own register "hippy autistic PLUR." This wing is why that phrase is coherent — the floor as regulation, belonging, and design law.
RECORD — cited
Stimming is regulation — per autistic adults themselves. Kapp et al. 2019 (32 autistic adults, via interviews and focus groups):
"Autistic adults highlighted the importance of stimming as an adaptive mechanism that helps them to soothe or communicate intense emotions or thoughts"
The study's own title carries the point: "People should be allowed to do what they like."
Kapp, Steward, Crane, Elliott, Elphick, Pellicano & Russell (2019), Autism — quote verbatim from the abstract
RECORD — cited
The floor, first person. DJ Mag's 2023 accessibility feature documents autistic ravers and researchers: Daryl Okene on the dance floor —
"latching onto pieces of freedom in public spaces, where I can usually feel quite socially anxious"
— and artist Gus: "To dance without inhibition — as though no one is watching." Dr Cathy Manning's design rule: ask autistic people what would help; that is how changes actually benefit them. Named venues exist: Spectrum at Dalston Superstore, collectives e-numbers and MAD.
DJ Mag, "How can we make clubs more accessible to autistic ravers?" (Rihana Osman, Sept 2023)
RECORD — cited
The scene runs neurodivergent-rich. DJ Mag reports an Association For Electronic Music survey: 58% of respondents working in electronic music demonstrated a neurodivergent condition (38% clinically diagnosed), against a general-population estimate of 15–20%.
DJ Mag, "Why are there so many neurodivergent people in electronic music?" (Harold Heath, Sept 2023) — figures as reported; the survey's raw report was not independently fetched
LORE — told as lore
"A rave is one big stim" — and its honest dissent. The telling (Wrong Planet forum, 2013, user metalab):
"It's like an entire social construct designed to revolve around and support extreme hyper sensitivity."
The same thread holds the variants that keep this card honest: other autistic users describe raves as overwhelming, and one warns of seizure risk from the lights. The floor is sanctuary for some — the culture's job is making that a choice, not a gamble.
wrongplanet.net, "Raves, stimming paradise?" (2013) — named tellers, dissent preserved
RECORD — cited
Sanctuary is being built into the infrastructure. Glastonbury's Sensory Calm Spaces (Bristol charity Diverse UK): first tent 2022, four across the site by 2024 — adjustable lighting, weighted blankets, ear defenders, autism-trained staff. "It gives us somewhere to stop for a minute." In the US, KultureCity (founded 2014) certifies venues Sensory Inclusive, and Live Nation began certifying its venues with them.
ITV News West Country (June 2024); ABILITY Magazine on KultureCity × Live Nation
RECORD — cited
The flash laws — where rave lighting meets written design law. WCAG 2.3.1, normative verbatim: "Web pages do not contain anything that flashes more than three times in any one second period…" The UK HSE's club guidance caps strobes at 4 flashes/second (as relayed by the Epilepsy Society); Epilepsy Action places the danger band at 10–25 flashes/second; UK broadcast rules hold the three-flash line. This estate obeys the same law: nothing on these pages flashes faster than 3 Hz — this museum's own comb cycles at 5.2 seconds, and every animation stops for prefers-reduced-motion.
W3C WCAG SC 2.3.1 (verbatim); Epilepsy Society / Epilepsy Action guidance (Epilepsy Society page verified via indexed text — direct fetch returns 403, declared honestly); Ofcom/ASA flashing-image rules

Ⅵ · WING VI · THE LIVING LAW — PLUR now, and the register it rules here

Thirty-plus years on, the four words are still working — and still being fought over. The last wing is the present tense.
RECORD — cited
The attendees are the headliners. Pasquale Rotella's stated ethos, from his underground days to EDC's half-million attendances: the true headliners at a rave are the crowd, not the booked artists — and EDC prints its lineups alphabetically, in equal-sized type, to make the point typographic. Insomniac sets up free kandi-making tables; Rotella collects kandi himself.
LA Weekly, "EDC 30" + "Inside the World of the Kandi Kids"
verify pass: the source uses "headliners" (lowercase, in quotes) for attendees — it does not establish capitalized "Headliners" as official branding. Displayed as the ethos it documents, not the trademark it doesn't.
RECORD — cited
The live tension: can four words be property? February 10, 2023 — Insomniac Holdings, LLC files US trademark application serial 97790066 for the word "PLUR," covering clothing. The community's recorded reaction: "That's not very PLUR of them." Frankie Bones — whom the coverage credits with popularizing PLUR, not coining it — publicly supported the filing. A Change.org petition, "PLUR is NOT for Sale" (June 15, 2025, Malorie Richards), asks the USPTO to reject it:
"PLUR — Peace, Love, Unity, Respect — is not a brand. It's a way of life. … You don't protect it — you restrict it."
This museum takes no side; it files the dispute itself as the exhibit. A word born from a mailing-list signature now has lawyers. The floor will decide what it always decides: what people actually say to each other with their hands.
