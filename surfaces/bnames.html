🛠 THE FULL WORKSHOP — every lane the registry really has (live ABI, 2026-08-22)

registeracc(registrant, domain_name, target) — reserve a name, pointing it at target. Fee 0.0000 today (live config); you carry the RAM (~live cost above), one-time and resellable. Term 365 days.
renew(owner, domain_name, days) — extend the term. All 13 current names expire 2027-08-01 (live read).
transfer(from, domain_name, to) — hand a name over, whole and on-chain. The trade lane's settlement primitive: offer → escrow → transfer.
setaccount(owner, domain_name, target) — re-point a name to a new account without moving ownership.
setchain(owner, domain_name, chain_key, address, addr_type, memo_tag) — the bleeding edge: one .b name resolving to an address on ANY registered chain — chain_key list rides the chainkeys table (addchainkey is admin). Names become universal handles.
release(owner, domain_name) — let a name go. The RAM row persists per the registry's design; cleanup is admin-side.

🌍 THE 10-BILLION-USER · 1,000-YEAR DESIGN — ruled after testing every option

We tested every architecture against the chain's real arithmetic (76 GB of buyable RAM, frozen supply, a 12.7M all-in ceiling) and only one way scales — the full analysis is docs/bdomain-scaling.md (nine agents, two independent derivations reconciling to the registry's own bytes):
TIER 1 — the on-chain anchor (paid, scarce) the domains row stays: ownership, expiry, transfer, release — all under require_auth. After the ruled trims the anchor is ~155 B/user (drop the byowner index, ordinal chain keys). Premium tier, millions of anchors, first-come allocation enforced by the chain itself.
TIER 2 — off-chain resolution (free, unbounded) addresses move behind a resolvers table (gateway + owner-set signer pubkey + TTL); responses carry timestamp + nonce + signature the client verifies against the on-chain key. Marginal on-chain cost per record: 0 bytes. Freshness native — deletion is instant, correction is mid-epoch. Where ENS landed too.
REJECTED — one line each buy-RAM-and-hope: the bytes do not exist past ~12.7M users, at any price · Merkle + Arweave/IPFS: cannot prove deletion — a stolen address stays valid all epoch, a loss class for a fund-routing registry · fully off-chain: forfeits require_auth, which is the whole contract.
THE LAW OF THE MIGRATION WINDOW evolving the live contract to the ruled shape costs under a dollar at 13 rows, a project at 13,000, impossible at 13 million. The setchain-per-address actions on this desk are today's live shape; the ruled design replaces that half with Tier-2 resolvers while the 13 anchors sail through unchanged. Ten billion users = ten billion keypairs, not accounts — only anchors take rows.
