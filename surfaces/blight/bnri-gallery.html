BNRi — the fleet's own art

the explorer renders every other project's inscriptions; this is ours — seven hexrect pieces, decoded and drawn in your browser from the packed format itself. the decoder reads both wire formats, v1 (8-byte records) and v2 (versioned, palette-indexed, run-length), detected by the version byte, never assumed. every pixel is computed here; nothing is a stored image.

the collection — decoded in front of you

each piece draws its rects in file order as it enters the viewport — that is the decoder working, not an effect; a piece that fails renders amber and says why. the build-up honours prefers-reduced-motion by drawing instantly.

the mirror law — how the art travels

the manifest mirrors the hex so the page is one request; the .hex files are the art and stay beside it, untouched — each survives on its own. copy the directory, not just this page, and the collection carries on a USB stick. the manifest's hex is byte-length-checked against the files at build time (221,175 chars today — 110,587 B of packed art in seven files).

the decoder reads the version byte first: a v2 blob starts 0x02; v1 begins with a coordinate byte for a real piece (row 0), never 0x02 as a version. we try v2 when byte0 is 2, else v1 — and a misparse renders as a declared failure, never as wrong pixels.
