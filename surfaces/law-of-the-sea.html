Law of the Sea

Every data flow on the estate is named in the language of the sea.

Founder ruling, 2026-09-21: “hold hard to naval terms with data flow and management. Law of the sea.”

  1. A data action gets the sea's word for it when one exists. The first is manifest: a file is added to the manifest before it sails to Autonomi.
  2. The word must be true to its meaning at sea. A term that only sounds naval is not used.
  3. A stranger must still understand the page. The naval word is the name; one plain sentence beside it says what happens.
ruled now

Manifest

The list of files a person has chosen to send: name, size, fingerprint, status — declared before they sail.

Why the sea

The sea solved the estate's problems first. A ship moves valuable cargo between strangers, across waters nobody owns, with no central authority on board. Sailors answered that with manifests, logs, watches, shared-loss rules and a duty to rescue. Those are the same answers a serverless data network needs.

        The glossary — only Manifest is ruled

        A first draft of the working vocabulary, in the order a file travels. Only manifest is ruled; every other row is proposed.

        Manifest ruled

        at sea The list of everything the ship carries, declared before sailing

        on the estate The list of files a person has chosen to send: name, size, fingerprint, status

        Cargo proposed

        at sea The goods being carried

        on the estate The files themselves

        Hold proposed

        at sea The space below decks where cargo waits and rides

        on the estate Local staging on the person's own device, before anything leaves

        Bill of lading proposed

        at sea The receipt a carrier gives for cargo taken aboard, stating what and for whom

        on the estate The price quote and payment plan a person sees before paying

        Clearance proposed

        at sea Permission from the port to sail

        on the estate The person's own authorization and spending ceiling

        Pilot proposed

        at sea The local expert who guides a ship through a harbor, never its owner

        on the estate The bridge program: it prepares the passage and holds no keys

        Make sail proposed

        at sea Departure

        on the estate The upload, once the wallet has paid

        Log proposed

        at sea The ship's written record, kept every watch, never erased

        on the estate The receipt trail: address, chunks, amount paid, time

        Port of call proposed

        at sea Where cargo is delivered

        on the estate The network a file lands on: Autonomi, Arweave, Hive, local

        Landfall proposed

        at sea First sight of the destination

        on the estate The datamap address coming back

        Sounding proposed

        at sea Measuring the depth before moving forward

        on the estate Verify by downloading and comparing the fingerprint

        Watch proposed

        at sea The crew on duty, in rotation, so the ship is never unattended

        on the estate Seats on shift; checks that run on every change

        Muster proposed

        at sea Calling the roll to confirm everyone is accounted for

        on the estate The registry count: every surface present and numbered

        Colors proposed

        at sea The flag that says whose ship this is

        on the estate Signed identity on a message or commit

        Ballast proposed

        at sea Weight carried for stability, not for sale

        on the estate Redundant copies kept so data survives

        Jettison proposed

        at sea Throwing cargo overboard to save the ship

        on the estate Deleting what can be deleted; never possible for permanent public data

        General average proposed

        at sea All parties share the loss when cargo is jettisoned to save the voyage

        on the estate Shared cost and shared loss across the hive, never one member alone

        Salvage proposed

        at sea Recovering a ship or cargo in peril

        on the estate Resuming a paid but unfinished upload without paying twice

        Dry dock proposed

        at sea Out of the water for repair

        on the estate A surface taken offline to be fixed

        Shakedown proposed

        at sea A new ship's first trial run

        on the estate Tests against mocks before anything real is spent

        Commission proposed

        at sea A ship formally enters service

        on the estate A surface goes live, proven at its public address

        Sick bay proposed

        at sea Where the corpsman treats the crew

        on the estate Bug diagnosis and repair

        Ten thousand years of sailors

        People have crossed open water for more than ten thousand years, and nearly every rule the estate needs was written by them first. Dates below are approximate and written from general knowledge, not from sources opened for this draft; each row is to be checked against a source.

        About 11,000 BCE
        Obsidian from the island of Melos reaches mainland Greece, so people were already crossing open Aegean water
        Proof that seafaring is older than farming, writing or the city
        About 8000 BCE
        The Pesse canoe, a dugout found in the Netherlands, the oldest known boat
        The first surviving hull
        About 5000 BCE
        Reed boats sealed with bitumen sail the Persian Gulf
        Trade by sea between early settlements
        About 3000 BCE onward
        Austronesian voyagers spread from Taiwan across the Pacific and Indian oceans
        Wayfinding by stars, swells and birds, with no instruments
        About 2500 BCE
        The Khufu ship is buried beside the Great Pyramid
        Plank-built ships made to last for eternity
        About 1300 BCE
        The Uluburun ship sinks off Turkey carrying copper, tin, glass and ivory from many lands
        The oldest mixed cargo we can still inventory: a manifest on the seabed
        About 1200 to 300 BCE
        Phoenician traders work the whole Mediterranean and pass the alphabet along their routes
        Trade networks that carried writing itself
        About 800 BCE onward
        The Rhodian sea law: cargo thrown overboard to save a ship is a loss shared by all
        General average, still in force today
        About 1000 CE
        Norse sailors reach North America
        Open-ocean crossing in the North Atlantic
        About 1100 to 1300 CE
        Polynesian voyagers settle Hawaii, Rapa Nui and Aotearoa
        The largest settlement by sea in human history
        12th to 14th century
        The Rolls of Oleron and the Consolato del Mare set out the rights and duties of masters and crews
        Written law of the sea, carried from port to port
        1405 to 1433
        Zheng He's treasure fleets sail from China to East Africa
        Fleet logistics on a scale Europe would not match for centuries
        1747
        James Lind's trial aboard HMS Salisbury shows citrus treats scurvy
        Often called the first controlled clinical trial, run at sea by a ship's surgeon
        1760s
        John Harrison's chronometer solves longitude
        A ship can know where it is
        1898
        The United States Navy Hospital Corps is established on 17 June
        The corpsman becomes a rating of his own
        1982
        The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea is signed
        The high seas are held in common and belong to no one state
        The founder in the line

        The founder joined that line himself: he served as a United States Navy and Fleet Marine Force Hospital Corpsman. He joined the Navy at 17 and left for his first boot camp in 1995. That is what he shares publicly; units, stations and operations stay out of this document.

        A Hospital Corpsman is the sailor who keeps the crew alive. A Fleet Marine Force corpsman does it ashore with the Marines, who call him "Doc" and call "Corpsman up" when someone is hit. The rating descends from the ship's surgeons and loblolly boys of the age of sail, the same tradition that produced Lind's scurvy trial.

        The estate already runs on his two trades. The operating room gives it seats, a surgical team and S.O.A.P. notes for every bug. The ship gives it the manifest, the log and the watch. The corpsman is where the two meet: one person, trained to act alone, far from any hospital, responsible for everyone aboard.

        That is the posture the estate asks of every seat. Treat what is in front of you, write it in the log, and never leave a shipmate behind.
