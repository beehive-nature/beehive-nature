THE CORRIDOR OF CHOKEPOINTS

eight rooms, one history: every leap in reaching an audience has been followed by someone new standing between the artist and the money.

THE PRINTER — movable type, circa 1440–1450, made copying a book cheap for the first time in Europe. Printing was unprofitable without distribution — a press with no way to reach readers was capital with nowhere to go.

Ada Palmer, UChicago · History.com

JOHANNES GUTENBERG invented movable type and died penniless; his own creditors impounded his presses before his invention ever paid him back.

History.com

THE STATIONER — England's Stationers' Company received a royal charter in 1557: a national print monopoly whose own Register recorded rights for stationers, not for authors. By 1586 the Star Chamber confined all English printing to three cities.

Britannica · Oxford Research Encyclopedia

THE BOOKSELLER — the Statute of Anne, 1710, was the first copyright law. Most authors still sold their work outright to booksellers, and its benefit to authors was, in its own era's word, incidental.

copyrighthistory.org · Wikipedia

THE LABEL — after ‘Crazy Blues’ sold roughly a million copies in 1920, labels built fortunes on Black artists largely excluded from ASCAP, where royalties went unenforceable.

GRAMMY.com

BESSIE SMITH, the Empress of the Blues, made Columbia Records millions of dollars and was never paid royalties on her recordings.

GRAMMY.com

MARY WELLS took ‘My Guy’ to number one in 1964. She died in poverty in 1992, at forty-nine, with no health insurance.

GRAMMY.com

THE DJ — payola. House hearings in November 1959 found 335 disc jockeys had admitted to accepting more than $263,000 in what was recorded as ‘consulting fees’ to play records.

Performing Songwriter · NPR

ALAN FREED, the DJ credited with popularizing the phrase ‘rock and roll,’ was fired over payola, pleaded guilty, and died broke and bitter in 1965.

Performing Songwriter · NPR

THE STREAM — a stream pays roughly four tenths of a cent. In 2024, about twelve million uploaders competed for it; 1,450 artists cleared a million dollars while Spotify paid a record ten billion dollars to rights holders.

Spotify Loud & Clear · CNBC · Spotify Royalties Guide

TAYLOR SWIFT's original masters sold to Shamrock Capital in November 2020 for $405 million total consideration. She re-recorded her catalog, then repurchased her masters outright for roughly $360 million in May 2025.

Music Business Worldwide · Billboard

THE AI TRAINER — Andersen v. Stability AI survived partial dismissal in August 2024; Getty Images v. Stability AI in the UK, decided November 2025, largely rejected the copyright claim. The question is not settled. This room prints it as unresolved, because it is.

Artnet

THE COLLAPSED SOLUTION — NFT resale royalties once looked like the fix. OpenSea made them optional in February 2023 and ended enforcement by February 2024. No court has ever held an on-chain resale royalty to be an enforceable right; what creators still receive, they receive by voluntary honor alone.

Art Newspaper
