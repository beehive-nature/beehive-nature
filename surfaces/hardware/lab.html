      the whole lab

      The Lab — science and art, under stage lights

      Tom's Hardware raised a generation of builders by testing everything and printing the numbers. This is that, for the sovereign age, with a light show. Every device gets two reviews published together: THE SCIENCE — automated, reproducible benches run by the BNR surgical code team, every number regenerable from an open harness — and THE ART — one human's lived verdict: does the ceremony feel sovereign, and does it survive a festival night? Science without art is a spec sheet. Art without science is an ad.
      The ethics law, printed where everyone can see it.
      Review units come from manufacturers and are disclosed on every review — gifts never buy verdicts; the disclosure proves it. No paid placement, no affiliate links, ever; every link is the same one a stranger would find. Only open tech gets a comb — closed boxes are indexed once, with reasons, on the guide page. Negative results publish, with the data, and vendors get right-of-reply appended verbatim. Scores are versioned to firmware, never evergreen.

      The Comb Score — six cells, no fake total

      Six axes, one hexagon, in bComb's own geometry. Five cells are computable; the sixth belongs to a human, and no average hides it: ⬡ Openness · ⬡ Air-gap · ⬡ Custody law · ⬡ Build · ⬡ Art · ⬡ Ecosystem fit. A device can bench perfectly and still die in the Art cell — that verdict is data too.
