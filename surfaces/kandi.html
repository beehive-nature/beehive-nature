🍬 the kandi bar

the kandi bar — make it, name it, gift it, cross it, show it
This is the mUseUm's ritual wing, alive — a social page with no inbox. String beads, spell a word, name who it's for, then gift it, cross it, or show it. Each one hands you a short line of text and you carry it in whatever chat you already use, so there is no account here and nobody to sign up with. Your arms live in this browser's own storage and no kandi is ever uploaded. Two things the page does by itself: it asks a public chain node whether the chain is up — that is the badge at the bottom, and it sends no kandi and no name, though that node does see your address — and if you pick a language other than English it fetches the translation file from this same site. Your first kandi is already waiting on your left arm.
make it. name it. do the handshake. peace, love, unity — and on respect, the kandi crosses. right arm trades, left arm keeps, and the crossing doesn't move until something lands in your hand. no feed, no room, nobody's inbox — the string goes hand to hand down whatever channel you two already share, and the moment it lands it's theirs. what somebody made FOR you never re-trades: no gift button, no crossing button, ever, on that arm — that much really is code. and a string is a copy, not a token: once you've handed it over you can't un-hand it, which is exactly why the piece leaves your arm when you give it.
Format: KND1|maker|for|ts36|beads|fnv — beads as [0-9] color indices and [A-Z] letter beads; FNV-1a/32 over the payload, base36. A checksum, not a signature — it catches a mistyped string, not a forged one, and a maker name is a claim, never an attestation. "zero network" is deleted from this page rather than softened, because two requests are page-initiated beyond its own scripts: rails-badge.js POSTs {} to eos.api.eosnation.io (falling back to eos.greymass.com) about 1.3 s after load with no user action — it carries no kandi and no soul name, but your IP, User-Agent and the timing of the visit reach a third party — and lang.js GETs lang-corpus.json same-origin, only when your language is not English. State is localStorage only (bkandi written here; the riders own bregister, blang, btranslated_pref, bnr_rails_pin), no cookies, no sessionStorage, no IndexedDB, and no XHR, WebSocket, sendBeacon or analytics tag anywhere in the chain. Gift, cross and show all terminate in the OS clipboard — the carrying is done by the person, and "show it" copies a #k= fragment, not a query, so a shared piece never enters the host's request log; it does still put the maker and made-FOR names in the opener's address bar and history, because the piece IS its seed. Arriving with a fragment only pre-fills the receive box and never writes storage. No DM transport, no relay, no inbox, no video: open, not built, and said plainly. Names are validated to a strict charset and rendered as text, never markup. Digital kandi copies — the culture's answer is the retirement rule, so gifting removes the piece from your arm and keeps a memory line, the same shape as the corpus's withdrawal law.

⚗ THE COMPOSER — string it

Beads: tap colors, or type a word — letters become letter-beads. Then name it: who you are (optional), and who it's for (only if it's made for someone — the dedication rides with it forever). Either way it strings onto your right arm, because it is yours until you gift it; the made-FOR law binds the copy they receive.

🫱🫲 THE THREE ARMS — right trades, left keeps, the crossing swaps

The etiquette, enforced as written in the mUseUm: tradeables ride the right arm; keepsakes and gifts received ride the left and never re-trade; never ask for a specific piece; and what you gift leaves your arm — a memory line stays, history kept.

THE CROSSING — offered, and only for another piece
Some kandi you would never give away. Offer it to the crossing instead: it stays yours until somebody puts a piece in your hand, and then the two swap at once. Nothing leaves your arm for nothing.
on respect, the kandi crosses. this is the arm for the piece you would never hand over — it does not move until something lands. what comes back rides the left arm, forever, because it was given to you.
Mechanics, stated plainly: a crossing is TWO one-way handoffs the page performs locally, not an escrow — there is no third party and nothing to trust but the floor. Your side completes at the instant you paste theirs: your piece retires to the memory line and theirs is written to S.left with rcv:true, so it can never re-trade. Same KND1 string, same FNV-1a/32 checksum, same retirement rule as the gift — a copy you already handed over cannot be un-handed, which is why the piece leaves your arm rather than being marked "traded".
