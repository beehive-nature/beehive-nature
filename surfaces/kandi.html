🍬 the kandi bar

the kandi bar — make it, name it, gift it, cross it, show it
This is the mUseUm's ritual wing, alive — a social page with no inbox. String beads, spell a word, name who it's for, then gift it, cross it, or show it. Each one hands you a short line of text and you carry it in whatever chat you already use, so there is no account here and nobody to sign up with. Your arms live in this browser's own storage and no kandi is ever uploaded. The page makes no outside request at all unless you ask for one: the rails badge at the bottom shows a status checked when this page was built, and only reaches a public chain node if you tap its ↻ — which sends no kandi and no name, though that node does see your address. The one other request is the translation file, fetched from this same site if you pick a language other than English. Your first kandi is already waiting on your left arm.
make it. name it. do the handshake. peace, love, unity — and on respect, the kandi crosses. right arm trades, left arm keeps, and the crossing doesn't move until something lands in your hand. no feed, no room, nobody's inbox — the string goes hand to hand down whatever channel you two already share, and the moment it lands it's theirs. what somebody made FOR you never re-trades: no gift button, no crossing button, ever, on that arm — that much really is code. and a string is a copy, not a token: once you've handed it over you can't un-hand it, which is exactly why the piece leaves your arm when you give it.
Format: KND1|maker|for|ts36|beads|fnv — beads as [0-9] color indices and [A-Z] letter beads; FNV-1a/32 over the payload, base36. A checksum, not a signature — it catches a mistyped string, not a forged one, and a maker name is a claim, never an attestation. This page makes no outside request on load. Ruled 2026-08-26: rails-badge.js paints a status BAKED at build time from a real get_info read (head 516799778, 2026-08-26) and prints that bake's date, so a stale bake advertises its own staleness; it POSTs {} to eos.api.eosnation.io (falling back to eos.greymass.com) only when a reader taps ↻ — that request carries no kandi and no soul name, but your IP, User-Agent and its timing reach a third party, which is why it is now yours to choose. The only other request is lang.js GETting lang-corpus.json same-origin, and only when your language is not English. Receipt: a network capture of this page records zero cross-origin requests on load, and exactly one after the tap. State is localStorage only (bkandi written here; the riders own bregister, blang, btranslated_pref, bnr_rails_pin), no cookies, no sessionStorage, no IndexedDB, and no XHR, WebSocket, sendBeacon or analytics tag anywhere in the chain. Gift, cross and show all terminate in the OS clipboard — the carrying is done by the person, and "show it" copies a #k= fragment, not a query, so a shared piece never enters the host's request log; it does still put the maker and made-FOR names in the opener's address bar and history, because the piece IS its seed. Arriving with a fragment only pre-fills the receive box and never writes storage. No DM transport, no relay, no inbox, no video: open, not built, and said plainly. ⚗ THE COMPOSER — string it

Beads: tap colors, or type a word — letters become letter-beads. Then name it: who you are (optional), and who it's for (only if it's made for someone — the dedication rides with it forever). Either way it strings onto your right arm, because it is yours until you gift it; the made-FOR law binds the copy they receive.
Letters are white beads, like the real thing — tap a colour to thread one in, or turn on thread colour and every word comes out alternating.

THE CROSSING — offered, and only for another piece
Some kandi you would never give away. Offer it to the crossing instead: it stays yours until somebody puts a piece in your hand, and then the two swap at once. Nothing leaves your arm for nothing.
on respect, the kandi crosses. this is the arm for the piece you would never hand over — it does not move until something lands. what comes back rides the left arm, forever, because it was given to you.
Mechanics, stated plainly: a crossing is TWO one-way handoffs the page performs locally, not an escrow — there is no third party and nothing to trust but the floor. Your side completes at the instant you paste theirs: your piece retires to the memory line and theirs is written to S.left with rcv:true, so it can never re-trade. Same KND1 string, same FNV-1a/32 checksum, same retirement rule as the gift — a copy you already handed over cannot be un-handed, which is why the piece leaves your arm rather than being marked "traded".
