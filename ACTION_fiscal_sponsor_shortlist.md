## 1. THE FRAMING DECISION — make this one before you contact anyone

**Lead with the data commons. Do not lead with hemp.**

Many fiscal sponsors carry blanket policies against cannabis-adjacent projects, driven by their banking relationships rather than by the merits. **A reflexive decline costs you weeks and you never find out it was reflexive.**

**The accurate description of this project is:**

> **An open food-composition data commons.** It commissions complete, lot-level analytical panels to a published specification and releases every result openly — including unfavourable ones. **The first commodity is hulled hemp seed**, a federally legal food ingredient that USDA prices weekly as an ordinary agricultural commodity and that FDA has GRAS-notified. **The schema generalises to any food.**

**That is not spin — it is the more accurate description.** The hemp numbers are entry #1; the schema is the deliverable. Leading with the plant misrepresents your own project *downward*.

**And put the hemp question directly in the first email**, so a policy exclusion surfaces in day one rather than week six:

> *"Do you have any policy restriction on projects involving industrial hemp? To be specific: hemp seed as a food ingredient, federally legal under the 2018 Farm Bill, FDA GRAS-notified (GRN 765, 771, 778), priced weekly by USDA AMS. No cannabinoid products, no THC products, no state-licensed cannabis activity."*

## 3. THE QUESTIONS TO ASK EVERY CANDIDATE

1. **Hemp policy** — §1 wording, first email.
2. **Fee.** Industry range is **8–15% of project revenue**, higher on some grant types. **Budget for it in the RFQ arithmetic** — it comes off the top of every grant.
3. **Can you hold and administer laboratory service contracts** on the project's behalf, and who signs?
4. **Data ownership and licence** — can the project publish results under an open licence, with the sponsor holding no restriction on release? **This is non-negotiable: C1 requires unconditional publication.**
5. **Advisory-body accommodation** — can an external group (the DAO) make recommendations that staff bring to your board?
6. **Grant eligibility support** — will you serve as applicant of record for federal programmes (USDA AFRI etc.)?
7. **Exit terms** — when the 1023 determination lands, how do assets, contracts and data transfer to the new entity? **Ask this before signing, not after.**

## 4. THE HIDDEN BENEFIT — sponsorship answers Q1 for you, temporarily

**Counsel question Q1 was the novel one: who holds fiduciary duty, the board or the senary circles?**

**Under fiscal sponsorship, the sponsor's board holds it. Full stop.** The DAO is straightforwardly advisory, because it has no other option.

**Which means you get to run the recommend-then-decide model in real conditions, for real money, before you have to formalise it in your own bylaws.** By the time the determination letter arrives you will have evidence about how the circles actually behave when a real commissioning decision is on the table — and the bylaws can encode what worked rather than what you guessed.

**That is worth more than the tax status.** Take the sponsorship period as a governance rehearsal, not just a funding bridge.
