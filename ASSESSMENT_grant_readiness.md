## 2. WHY AFRI IS HARD RIGHT NOW — said plainly

**AFRI reviewers are academic scientists scoring scientific merit.** They look for: a hypothesis-driven question, a PI with a publication record, institutional research capacity, and **preliminary data.**

**What you are missing, listed without softening:**

- **No PI with a research track record.** This is the single biggest one.
- **No institutional research infrastructure** (a c3 in formation does not read as research capacity).
- **No preliminary data.** You propose to measure; you have not yet measured.
- **No publications in the field.**
- **As written, it reads as data collection rather than research** — and AFRI Foundational wants a question, not an inventory.

**That is the honest read. It is also fixable, and one of the fixes is free.**

---

## 3. THE FREE FIX — you have a real research question and you have been treating it as a byproduct

**You have been pitching an open data commons. For a science reviewer, that is infrastructure, and infrastructure scores badly against hypothesis-driven work.**

**But this is sitting in your own materials:**

> **Does hulled hemp seed's mineral content survive its phytate load — and does that vary by origin and cultivar?**

**That is a genuine, novel, publishable question.** Hemp hearts are marketed on mineral density — 167% DV magnesium, 132% phosphorus, 90% zinc. **Phytate at ~4 g/100 g plausibly compromises the iron and zinc.** And **nobody has paired a complete mineral panel with phytate on the same lot, let alone across three jurisdictions.**

**Reframe the application:** the bioavailability question is the **science**; the open dataset is the **deliverable**. Same work, same budget, entirely different scoring — because now you are testing whether a widely-published nutritional profile means what it appears to mean.

**Secondary question, also real:** GRAS 765 asserts *"no known anti-nutritional properties."* Your spec includes trypsin inhibitor activity. **You are directly testing an incumbent federal filing's claim.** Reviewers like that.

**This costs nothing and it is the highest-leverage change available.**

## 4. THE PREP LIST, RANKED BY LEVERAGE

### 4a · Recruit an academic PI — biggest structural lever

**And you have a pipeline you have already read.** The USDA Hemp Descriptor Handbook acknowledges roughly fifty named hemp researchers — **Cornell's hemp group (Larry Smart, Jacob Toth, George Stack), Anya Osatuke as co-editor, Zachary Stansell at USDA-ARS**, plus contributors across a dozen land-grant institutions.

**These people already care about standardised hemp characterisation — that is what the handbook is.** You are proposing to extend their own schema into composition. **That is the warmest possible approach: not "fund my project" but "your descriptor set has no complete composition panel behind it; I have written the spec; would you look at it?"**

**Tradeoff, stated honestly:** a land-grant co-PI usually means **the university becomes the applicant and holds the award**, not your c3. **Better odds, less control.** For a first grant that is often the correct trade — but decide it deliberately rather than discovering it at submission.

### 4b · Get preliminary data — self-fund one or two lots

**This converts "we propose to measure" into "we measured, here is what we found, and here is why it must scale."** In competitive review that difference is frequently decisive.

**One lot at full spec, published openly, also proves every governance claim you are making** — C1 publication, the schema, the GRIN-Global export. **It is simultaneously preliminary data and a working demonstration of the institution.**

### 4c · Get the quotes — nothing proceeds without a budget

Already in motion. **No application exists without a number.**

### 4d · Letters of support

Growers, processors, buyers who would use the data. **You have the farmer.** Two or three letters materially help mission-weighted programmes.

### 4e · Data management plan

Federal programmes require one. **You are ahead here** — repository, open licence, `trait_name` × PUID format, GRIN-Global submission path. Write it up; it is largely done.

## 5. WHAT YOU ALREADY HAVE THAT IS GENUINELY STRONG

**Do not undersell these — several are rare:**

1. **A documented gap, verified rather than asserted.** Most applications claim a gap exists. **You can demonstrate that no complete lot-level COA is publicly available anywhere** — searched, with the negative result recorded. That is an unusually strong opening.
2. **A complete, method-cited specification.** Most applicants propose to *develop* methods. **You have ISO 12966, AOAC numbers, LOQ gates and acceptance criteria already written.** Reviewers read that as competence.
3. **Alignment to an existing federal schema.** Reviewers reward interoperability, and you are extending USDA's own descriptor vocabulary rather than inventing one.
4. **A comparative three-jurisdiction design** — that is a real experimental structure, not a convenience sample.
5. **An unconditional publication commitment**, pre-registered. Increasingly valued and rarely offered.

## 6. WHERE TO AIM FIRST — order matters

1. **Farm Aid** — explicitly accepts fiscal-sponsored applicants, family-farmer mission, smaller amounts, better odds. **Good first win, and a funded track record helps every later application.**
2. **State agriculture / specialty crop block grants** — much less competitive than federal, and hemp qualifies in several states.
3. **Private foundations** — food systems, open data, agricultural transparency.
4. **USDA AMS Regional Food System Partnerships** — planning tier $100–250k is well-sized. **FY26 closed 5 June; target FY27.**
5. **AFRI** — **after** §4a and §4b. With a PI and preliminary data you are at or above the 20% baseline. Without them, below it.

## 7. THE STRAIGHT ANSWER TO YOUR QUESTION

**For fiscal sponsorship: yes, you have enough. Send the emails.**

**For a first grant at Farm Aid or state level: yes, once you have quotes and two letters.**

**For AFRI today: no — and applying now would spend a submission cycle to learn what this document already tells you.** With an academic PI and one lot of preliminary data, that flips.

**The fastest path to "good odds" is not more writing. It is one email to a Cornell hemp researcher and one self-funded lot.** Those two moves change the category you are competing in.
