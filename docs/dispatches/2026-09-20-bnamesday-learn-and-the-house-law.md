## The addition

- **Every foreign word opens.** A house of the thaksa table opens to its letters *by the names a Thai child learns
  them* — ก ไก่ *ko kai*, chicken … **ผ ผึ้ง *pho phueng*, bee** (Jupiter's group). 44 consonants in the core, all 41
  grouped ones asserted named. Days, colours, planets, postures and houses all carry how to say them and what they mean.
- **Go deeper.** 28 curated links (the calendar's own source, Raduraksti, the colours of the day, the postures, the
  Thai script, the nine planet-gods, the thaksa paper, the zodiac and each animal, stems, branches, pillars, the five
  phases, the solar terms, the Yì Jīng, **each of the 64 hexagrams to its own entry**, Shao Yong, the trigrams, the
  Human Design System). Every one checked alive (HTTP 200) on 2026-09-20; one helper builds them all —
  `target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"`, and the page says each opens a new tab. Followed only by a reader's tap:
  the page itself still makes two same-origin GETs and nothing else.
- **"+n more names" is a door**, not a number: the whole day opens as text — calendar names, the long list, the 22 May
  note — every name a button, then the ways deeper and the estate's own school (university · blanguage · profile).
- **The hub never clips**: three names at most, the rest counted (founder's screenshot: five names pushed the date out).
- **Translation, honestly scoped.** Native-script words are marked `translate="no"`, so a browser's translate carries
  the prose into a reader's tongue and leaves Latvian, Thai and Chinese as written. NOT done: the book's dictionary
  law (`every word a key… whole sentences with {n} slots`). This page's copy is still inline English; keying it is the
  next lane and the precondition for the estate's 28 tongues here.
