## 1. THE d.s.p. DOCTRINE — corrected to a METHOD, not an interpretation

**Withdrawn:** v1's "a d.s.p. in CP/Inq context typically certifies no legitimate surviving heirs claiming the honour." That was a predetermined reading.
**Banked instead (founder ruling):** *"Died without issue" is a source assertion whose meaning must be established in that particular source* — which class of children it concerns (all / surviving / legitimate / heirs) is a finding, not an assumption. Where chronicle and charter accounts conflict (the Stuteville specimen: *Chronicle of Meaux* "all except Nicholas died childless" vs. charters showing Eustace's son Robert), **the conflict is recorded as a conflict; it is not itself an explanation.** Procedure: read the phrase's own source, note its genre and purpose, list what it does and does not exclude, and preserve competing hypotheses with sources.

## 2. WALTER de MANDEVILLE — the documentary picture, competing hypotheses preserved

New read (FMG Valoignes chapter) establishes the surrounding family **from primary records**:
- **Gunnor de Valoignes** (d. after 1208), daughter & only child of **Robert de Valoignes** (d. 1184) [his own Binham charter names "Gunore filie mee"], m. (2) after 1194 **Robert FitzWalter** of Woodham Walter, Essex (d. 1235), the Magna Carta baron.
- The **Bracton inquiry of 1234/35**: land descending to "**Cristiana de Mandevilla**… **ex parte Gunnore matris sue**"; her brother "**Walterus filii Roberti**" (Walter FitzRobert); the 1233 record of "**Christine, late the wife of W. Earl of Maundeville**," whose livery passed to Valoignes-line heirs (Lora de Balliol and sisters) — consistent with the corpus's Christine death-year 1232.
- So the corpus's **g26 "Christine FitzRobert" is document-anchored**: daughter of Robert FitzWalter × Gunnor de Valoignes; wife of **W[illiam], the last Earl of Essex (d. 8 Jan 1227)** — the same William whose d.s.p. started this question.

**Walter's parentage — hypotheses (sources preserved; none accepted or rejected):**
- **H1′ (provider, revised):** Walter = son of the last earl × Christine. The earl is recorded d.s.p.; his widow's 1233 livery and the 1234/35 heir structure show the estate flowing to Valoignes-line heirs, weighing against a surviving son — but that is context, not proof; the phrase's scope in ITS sources (CP basis still unread) is the open read. An unrecorded child remains a **possibility, not supporting evidence**.
- **H2 (fusion — now documentary):** the corpus's "Walter de Mandeville" fuses the attested **Walter FitzRobert (Christine's BROTHER**, Bracton 1234/35) with Christine's Mandeville marriage. Under H2 the g25←g26 edge mis-files a brother as a son-in-law-line.
- **H3 (cadet):** unattached Mandeville cadet; no affirmative evidence.
**Verdict: g25←g26 UNRESOLVED-C** (affirmative parentage evidence for Walter missing); g24←g25 rides on Walter. The method note stands: nothing here is accepted or rejected on the d.s.p. phrase alone. The branch's defensible descent already stops at the earlier contradictory **g20→g21** edge; this is a later defect, not the first frontier.

## 4. DNA WORDING — qualified

**Withdrawn:** v1's "mismatch ⇒ the Bath-origin tradition is contradicted for that tested line."
**Corrected:** a mismatch **could challenge the proposed shared paternal line**, but it could equally be explained by **a documentary error in either tested line's documented descent, or an unexpected parentage event (NPE) anywhere along either line**. No numeric NPE prior is asserted here because this repository does not yet carry a resolvable source for one. A single mismatch is therefore not a verdict about the 17th century.
