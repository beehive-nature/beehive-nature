## 1 · What this is: the second product of the language lane

BlanguageDOCK (BRIEF-04, zCode-R steward) is the **corpus** — every user-visible string,
every language, docked permissionless. This dispatch names its sibling: **translated `.b`
resolution** — a `.b` name rendered, searched, and resolved in the reader's own language
(`alice.b` reachable through its attested translation), with bLOVErAi as the
interpretation layer. The founder's stated win: **availability** — ten billion people
whose mother tongue isn't English can hold and speak names that are theirs.

## 2 · THE COLLISION, NAMED (escalated, not resolved — both texts are the founder's)

- **BlanguageDOCK's ruled thesis** (BRIEF-04 §0, verbatim): *"every language — tribal,
  indigenous, rare, endangered, or the boring global ones — docks without asking anyone…
  no gatekeeper decides worthiness."* And its own moral: *"A language dying is not a
  market segment that failed to justify itself."*
- **The new rule**: ≥300 million mother-tongue speakers gates `*.b`/`*b-translated`
  registration.

These are compatible **only as two layers with two laws**: the corpus stays gateless
(any language docks, contributes, withdraws); the **naming alias layer** carries a
threshold because it touches money-routing and the confusable attack surface. That split
may be exactly the intent — the threshold as a *security parameter*, not a worthiness
judgment. But written law currently says gateless, and a 300M gate excludes every one of
the ~2,279 endangered languages the brief exists to honor. **Gate T-1: is the split the
intent, and is 300M the number?** (For scale: roughly a dozen languages clear 300M native
speakers; ~40 clear 100M. The gate's number decides whether this is "the major scripts
first" or "only the giants ever.")

## 3 · The exploit map (the founder's instinct is right — here is the surface)

1. **Confusable/homograph amplification.** The naming layer's uniqueness already rests on
   the confusable-skeleton law (UTS-46 + skeleton, one canonical authority). Translated
   aliases multiply the surface: a Cyrillic or CJK alias visually confusable with an
   existing Latin name is the classic wallet-drainer. **The existing answer extends:
   every alias enters the SAME skeleton tree as every name** — one tree, one authority,
   cross-script skeletons — or aliases don't exist. No parallel per-language namespace.
2. **Withdrawal griefing — the founder's "tribes that can remove their language."**
   BlanguageDOCK's ruled doctrine: withdrawal is honored, history kept. Applied naively
   to naming, an adversary "representing" a language registers its lane, names
   accumulate, then a withdrawal breaks every alias — or worse, the lapsed lane gets
   re-registered by a squatter who inherits the traffic.
3. **Representation capture.** Who speaks FOR a language? A 300M-speaker language has no
   single owner; a tribal language may have a legitimate council. Both directions are
   exploitable: fake councils claiming small languages; nobody-in-particular claiming
   giant ones.
4. **Measurement.** Speaker counts need a Tier-R source (reproducible by any stranger).
   Ethnologue is proprietary/paywalled — it fails Tier R. UNESCO's atlas covers
   endangerment, not size. **The gate's own number currently has no lawful ruler to
   measure it with.**

## 4 · The design principle that defuses most of it: ALIASES ARE POINTERS, NEVER OWNERSHIP

The pointers law, applied to naming: **a translated alias never owns anything.** It is a
rendering/resolution hint bound to the canonical skeleton entry — the same shape as the
DM law ("a message is a POINTER, never content") and the same fail-closed rule 10:

- Resolution through an alias ALWAYS terminates at the canonical name's leaf and its
  Merkle proof; an alias carries no keys, no records, no value.
- **Withdrawal then lapses rendering, never resolution**: the alias stops displaying
  (first-class withdrawal state, receipt-shaped, per BRIEF-04), the canonical name is
  untouched, and nothing an adversary does to a language lane can move or break funds.
  Rule 10 holds: a lapsed alias REFUSES rather than falling back to a guess.
- Re-registration of a lapsed lane cannot inherit anything, because there is nothing to
  inherit — pointers don't accumulate equity.

Under this principle the withdrawal exploit collapses from "breaks the namespace" to
"stops a courtesy rendering" — which is exactly the blast radius a language community
SHOULD control.

## 5 · The bAccord/escrow shape (deeply considered, as ordered — options, not design)

- **Language-lane registration = a bAccord** between the registering attestors and the
  registry: terms on the wall, bQueenBee-refereed (the farmers.html pattern), with a
  **stake escrowed against representation fraud** — slashed on a proven false-council
  claim, returned on clean operation.
- **Lapse windows, not instant death**: withdrawal opens a redemption-window cadence
  (the 28-day pawnbroker ancestor in b-collateral-lending) during which affected users
  see the withdrawal receipt before rendering stops — no rug-pull UX even when the
  withdrawal is legitimate.
- **Escrow never touches the canonical layer**: per §4, the accord governs the alias
  lane only. A community's worst-case power is over its own language's rendering —
  never over anyone's identity or funds. (Article V.1 holds: lane registrants fund
  their own lane's storage; the hive subsidizes nothing.)
