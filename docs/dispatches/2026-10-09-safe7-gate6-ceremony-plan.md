# 2026-10-09 — Safe 7 gate-6 ceremony plan (handoff §5 step 5; authorizes nothing)

## 0 · For the founder: one page

**What this is.** A session where you are present while a Safe 7 runs, for the first time, a hardware image that carries the bpq app, and the device's own post-quantum card, binding and file signature are checked against two oracles that are not the device, while every refusal the emulator already showed is shown again on glass. It is the audit's gate 6; it authorizes nothing, and nothing in it runs until you have made the choices below and named the day.

**Four decisions only you make.**
1. **Which device.** Choose A, the Safe 7 you use: its seed is erased, its factory attestation ends for good, and Suite may refuse it afterwards (unknowable until done). Two standing dispatches (the 10-04 handoff and the optical lane) say in so many words not to flash this device; neither is a ruling, so choosing A is your ruling over that text, recorded in the receipt. Choose B, a second Safe 7 bought for this: one device's price, and your daily device is untouched. (Choosing neither means gate 6 stays open and nothing on-device is ever measured.)
2. **Accept that unlocking ends attestation forever on that unit.** Choose yes: the image can be installed and the ceremony runs. Choose no: the device refuses any non-Trezor image at install, and this lane ends at the emulator receipt already in hand. Inside this choice, one more: whether a last authenticity check is taken before the unlock. Taken: the estate holds the only attestation record that unit will ever give; it needs a host that drives a stock Safe 7 over its cable, which has never been exercised here (the audit's gate 2). Not taken: "attestation remains intact" can never be receipted.
3. **Fixture seed or the device's own seed.** Choose the fixture (the public test vector): the device card (its id and three public keys) is expected to equal the emulator's, as the second emulator profile did, and the oracles can recompute its keys from the public seed; whether its signatures match the emulator's byte for byte is not receipted anywhere; the identity is public and worthless, the device is wiped at the end, and it needs your reading of one spec sentence that says no recovery phrase goes onto the device against the ruling that names only the bzDiD phrase. Choose your own seed, made on the device by your hands: a real device identity; the oracles verify its signatures but can never recompute its keys; recovery is a wipe and a re-entry by your hands; a passphrase, if you enable one, changes the identity; and you name the wallet id it binds to, by your own wallet press, or it binds to nothing.
4. **Debug image or reproducible non-debug image.** Choose debug: this plan's preflight refuses it, because the pinned two-build recipe only makes a non-debug image and the install act accepts only the digest that recipe named; a debug ceremony needs a second reviewed build wrapper and a revised plan, neither of which exists or is designed here. A debug image also carries the pairing-secret debug hole the optical lane says no shipped Safe 7 build may carry; it is expected to wipe its seed on every boot (so the fixture would be host-loaded after each boot, and your own seed is not offered on it); it can read the screen's text through the debug link but cannot record its pixels; and it is not the named image after reproducible build evidence that gate 6 asks for. Choose non-debug: the image is the one the two-build receipt names; every seed act is the device's own flow by hand, and every screen is witnessed by your eyes.

**Four more, with no default, listed at the end.** Which wallet id an own-seed device binds to; cable and host box (the device has never yet been reached from the shell the scripts run in); whether gate 6 runs before the audit's earlier gates that have no receipt (running now puts on a device a fork 641 commits off upstream and 723 behind it, with the security reconciliation unreceipted); who holds the unit afterwards. One optional test (leaving a confirm screen alone until the device locks itself) costs ten minutes of an hour and is yours to keep or drop.

**What your hands do.** Put the device in bootloader mode; press Unlock on its red screen; compare the fingerprint under the install screen's info button with the one your agent names, then tap the checkmark, or the cross; tap through the red UNSAFE screen on every boot; read the six-digit pairing code off the screen so your agent can type it; create or enter the seed; read each confirmation page and hold to confirm, or cancel when the plan says cancel; say yes or no to what you see. Your agent runs every host step from scripts that were reviewed before the day; the unlock and the install are sent by those scripts and confirmed by your finger.

**What you never do.** Type a command. Type a seed word into any computer. Put the bzDiD phrase on the device. Import this device's backup, or anything derived from its seed, into a browser or wallet page. Put any value on this device. Hand a seed, passphrase or PIN to any agent.

**What it costs in time.** About one power-on hour per leg: a watchdog the bootloader arms for this image resets the device about 59 minutes 56 seconds after each boot into the firmware; no reload call exists in the tree and only the secure monitor can reach the counter. The hour starts at each boot into the firmware; the bootloader-mode acts (lock state, unlock, install) are not under it. Three firmware legs at most (pairing, seed and card; signatures and refusals; recovery and end), each resumable after a reset. Keep the cable's power connected throughout: on battery the device locks itself after 40 seconds, and a half-finished signing screen dies with it.

Say which, and the day. Nothing below runs until then.

## 4 · Abort rules

Each: stop; record the act number, the command, the unedited output and what the founder saw; no retry without a new receipt that names what changed.
- **Digest or fingerprint mismatch** at act 0 or 5: the file is not the named image; nothing is sent.
- **Oracle disagreement** (any check false in either oracle, or a check present in both oracles with different results in the two): the card or signature is not accepted; acts after it do not run.
- **A refusal that does not fire** (act 12, or a control at act 11): the device or an oracle accepted what it must refuse; the ceremony ends and the receipt says so.
- **A pairing prompt the founder cannot answer from the screen** (no code shown, or a prompt outside acts 1, 2, 6 and 13): stop; record it; the keyring question in §8 is the first suspect.
- **Watchdog reset mid-act**: the act is marked interrupted, not failed; resume per act 14, once; a second reset inside the same act is an abort.
- **The founder says stop**: stop at once, record the act and his word, wipe only if he says so.

## 6 · What this ceremony establishes, and does not

- **Exercised on hardware**, never "proved": the wording ceiling is "sound by construction / isolated by design" (CLAUDE.md §2 law 4; handoff `:128-129`; SPEC-BPQ-1 `:294-299`).
- **Fit numbers.** Signing time is measured for the first time (host-side round trip). On-device stack headroom and heap fragmentation have no readout on a non-debug image: the ceremony establishes only that N signatures returned without the allocation error the C path reports ("returns an error, not a crash", 10-05 receipt `hardware_fit.heap`); a readout is instrumentation nobody has designed and this plan does not design (ORDERS-1 `:61`).
- **No channel confidentiality.** The host link is classical Noise_XX THP (audit `:45`; SPEC-BPQ-1 `:297-298`): a device signature says who signed; it does not make the cable post-quantum.
- **Attestation lost, not gained.** Act 4 ends it for that unit; nothing here is device attestation (handoff `:129`).
- **A cross-check between implementations, not an audit** (`docs/bpq-device.md:100-102`; SPEC-BPQ-1 `:270-272`). Matching development images do not authorize installation (reproducible-build `:94-97`); only the founder's checkmark at act 5, on the day, will.
