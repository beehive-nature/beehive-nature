# RECEIPT — the panel undercount: the founder was right, and here is exactly how far it goes

**Founder's claim under test:** *"make sure they are not doing magic with the data/stats
like the 180 different cannabinoid possibillities in cannabis"* — i.e. a lab's "total
cannabinoids" is a sum of its own small panel, not a total.

**Verdict: CORRECT, and documentable from primary regulation rather than inference.**

---

## 1 · WHAT REGULATORS ACTUALLY REQUIRE — read from the regulations themselves

| authority | analytes required |
|---|---|
| Health Canada (Cannabis Regs SOR/2018-144 s.90) | **4** — THC, THCA, CBD, CBDA |
| Oregon | **4** |
| California (4 CCR 15724) | **6** — + CBG, CBN |
| Colorado (1 CCR 212-3-4-210) | **8** |
| New York | ~10 + a catch-all *"Any Other Marketed Phytocannabinoid"* — **note it scopes the panel by COMMERCE, not by chemistry** |
| AOAC Official Method 2018.11 | **12** validated |
| commercial "expanded" panels (SC Labs, Trichome, DRS) | **15–16**, the ceiling |
| USDA hemp compliance (7 CFR 990) | effectively **one analyte pair** (THC + 0.877×THCA) |

**Against ~160 cannabinoids identified in the plant.** So: **4–16 measured, ~160 known.**
A COA's "Total Cannabinoids" line is **a panel floor wearing a total's name** — precisely
the `NotMeasured` rendered as a number that this project refuses everywhere else. Third
instance of that error class caught this sprint, and this one is the founder's catch.

## 2 · THE COUNT ITSELF — and a widely-used number we must never cite

- **~160 minor cannabinoids identified** (Gandlevskiy, Barge & Cravotto, *Phytochemical
  Analysis* 2025, PMID 41321142) — the current figure.
- **125 isolated and structurally characterised** across 11 subclasses (Radwan et al.
  2021, the University of Mississippi inventory) — the stricter criterion.
- **"113" has NO primary source.** It is the most-quoted number in cannabis writing and a
  targeted trace found no publication behind it; the Mississippi series runs
  70 → 104 → 120 → 125. **Do not cite 113.** And "~150 detected" (Citti 2019) means
  detected, *"most of them have neither been isolated nor characterized"* — conflating
  detected with characterised is itself the same error class.

## 3 · THE GAP, DEMONSTRATED ON THE SAME SAMPLES

- **Monti et al. 2022** ran targeted (15 cannabinoids) *and* untargeted LC-HRMS on **the
  same 45 inflorescence samples**: the untargeted workflow found **19 additional
  compounds**, 9 confidently assigned to known cannabinoids.
- **Cerrato et al. 2025** annotated **61 phytocannabinoids** including full C1–C7 alkyl
  homologue series, cis/trans isomers, O-methylated derivatives and sesquicannabinoids —
  **whole structural families no commercial panel holds a standard for.**
- **Citti et al. 2019** found **THCP and CBDP in FM2, a government-produced standardised
  medicinal cannabis** — purely because nobody had looked for heptyl side chains. The
  cleanest possible statement of *what you do not measure, you do not find*.
- **Berman et al. 2018**: two extracts equally high in CBD (50% w/w) did **not** produce
  the same anticonvulsant effect — evidence that the unpanelled fraction is
  *functionally* consequential, not merely present. **This supports the founder's
  full-spectrum instinct on its own citations.**

## 4 · THE HONEST BOUNDARY — two limits that must ship with the finding

**(a) The gap is proven in COMPOUND COUNT, not in MASS.** No peer-reviewed work
quantifying what fraction of cannabinoid *mass* sits outside a panel could be found. The
widely-repeated *"10–30% of the mass of a cannabis extract remains unidentified on a
typical COA"* traces **only to trade press**, whose page returned HTTP 403 and carried no
primary citation. **Do not use that number.** The defensible claim is compound count and
structural-family coverage.

**(b) EVERY figure in this receipt describes inflorescence, plant material, resin or
extract. NONE describes hemp seed** — hull-on, dehulled, or oil. Transferring these
counts to a seed matrix is unsupported by any source here. So the undercount is real and
it does **not** rescue a seed figure: the seed's ceiling is set by the contamination
mechanism (no glandular trichomes on seed), not by how many analytes a lab runs. Both
findings stand together without contradiction.

**(c) There is essentially one non-targeted total-cannabinoid method** — the Fast Blue B
colorimetric assay (Jornet-Martinez et al., *Molecules* 2023) — and its own authors call
it a *"rough estimation"*, calibrated against CBD alone. The paper states flatly:
*"no reference methods are available for the estimation of the total content of
cannabinoids."* LC-CAD unified calibration is the most promising untried route.

## 5 · CONSEQUENCE FOR THE TREE

1. **A new standing display law:** the surfaces never render "total cannabinoids" without
   naming the panel size. `total (n analytes measured of ~160 known)` — the same shape as
   the fatty-acid disaggregation law and the `NotMeasured ≠ 0` law. Three instances now
   share one grammar.
2. **Beehive University's first course gets its worked example** — this receipt *is* the
   COA-reading lesson: the learner finds the undercount on real material.
3. **Gate CB-4:** does the project commission its own broad-spectrum assay on its
   commissioned COA — the one measurement that would let us publish a count nobody else
   publishes, on our own material, with the funding flag firing on ourselves?
