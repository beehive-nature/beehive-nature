## 2 · THE LETHALITY CLAIM — the defensible form, held to the same ceiling as security language

The house never lets a claim run stronger than its source. The precise, defensible
statements are:

- **No confirmed human death from cannabis overdose alone** — the commonly stated and
  broadly supported form.
- **No lethal dose was ever established in dogs or monkeys**: the classic oral-toxicity
  work failed to kill dogs or primates at the highest doses tested (grams per kilogram —
  orders of magnitude above any use). *Attribution from this seat's memory (the 1973
  oral-toxicity line); the exact citation is owed by the veterinary lane before this
  sentence renders on any surface. UNVERIFIED-pending-receipt.*
- **Rodent oral LD50 values exist** (hundreds to ~2,000 mg/kg range in rats). So the
  fully precise sentence is *"no established lethal dose in humans, dogs, or primates"* —
  not "no LD50 in any species." The stronger phrasing would hand a hostile reader a
  one-line refutation, which is the exact gift this project never gives.

**And the reconciliation that dissolves the apparent collision: non-lethal and
toxicosis are both true.** Canine THC toxicosis (the three PMIDs in `SPEC-PATH2SCALE-1`
§1) is a real and growing clinical class — ataxia, distress, incontinence — and it is
**rarely fatal**, with reported deaths typically involving co-ingestants (chocolate,
xylitol) or aspiration. The founder's ruling and zAgent's receipts describe the same
animal: a species that does not die from THC and genuinely suffers from it. **ACiD, not
lethality, is the correct safety currency — which is precisely what the ruling says.**
