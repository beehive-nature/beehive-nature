## 2 · THE ARITHMETIC THAT NEEDS NO SOURCE — the seed disagrees with itself

1. **18,250 acres × the quoted 3–6 t/acre = 54,750–109,500 t — not the printed
   36,500–73,000 t.** The printed range corresponds to **2–4 t/acre**, a rate the seed
   never states. One of the two numbers is wrong by construction.
2. **"x4 more than forests" does not follow from the seed's own quoted ranges.**
   Hemp 3–6 vs forests 0.8–2.4 t/acre spans **1.25× to 7.5×** — "4×" is a midpoint
   presented as a ratio.
3. **1.83 M acres × 3–6 t = 5.5–11.0 Mt; × 2–4 t = 3.7–7.3 Mt.** The printed
   8,212,500 t (= 4.49 t/acre) sits inside the first range and outside the second — the
   document uses inconsistent rates in consecutive paragraphs.

These three are checkable by any reader with a calculator; they are graded
**internally inconsistent** without touching an external source.

## 3 · WHAT THE SCIENCE RECEIPTS SAY — gross uptake is not sequestration

**The load-bearing receipt (fetched this session, PubMed E-utilities):**
**Telleria et al., *Sci Total Environ* 2022 — "From hemp grown on carbon-vulnerable lands
to long-lasting bio-based products" (PMID 35843325).** Hundred-year soil-organic-carbon
modelling + consequential and dynamic LCA of hemp on French carbon-vulnerable lands:

- Leaving hemp stems on the field builds **25.8 t/ha of soil carbon over 100 years**
  (≈0.26 t/ha/yr) — and that scenario **fails to compensate the emissions from diverting
  the prior land use**: gross biogenic uptake is not net mitigation.
- The net climate benefit of hemp comes from **carbon retained in long-lived products**
  (insulation boards, car panels, hemp concrete co-products) and **displacement of
  fossil-based products** — and arrives *"well beyond 2100"*, not in a season.
- Yield is the dominant uncertainty.

**Against that receipt, the 8–15 t CO2/ha figure is gross-uptake-shaped:** it is the
right order for how much CO2 a vigorous crop *photosynthesises into biomass in one
season*, and the wrong shape for how much stays out of the atmosphere. Presented as
"absorbs", it is **the panel-undercount error class once more — a number wearing a name
it did not earn** (there: "total" on a 4-analyte panel; here: "sequestered" on a seasonal
flux). A companion LCA class exists for the product side (e.g. *Materials* 2023,
environmental LCA of a hemp-based building material, PMID 38005138).

## 4 · THE LIVESTOCK 52% — off by ~3.5× against every FAO assessment

The 51–52% family traces to **Goodland & Anhang, "Livestock and Climate Change",
World Watch Magazine Nov/Dec 2009** — an essay whose 51% figure was specifically
contested by FAO authors and subsequent literature. The assessment record:
**Gerber et al. 2013, *Tackling Climate Change Through Livestock* (FAO): 14.5%** of
global anthropogenic GHG from livestock supply chains; FAO's 2023 *Pathways towards
lower emissions* revises toward **~12%**. Records:
<https://openknowledge.fao.org/3/i3437e/i3437e.pdf> ·
<https://openknowledge.fao.org/3/cc9236en/cc9236en.pdf> — **NOT fetched this session**
(PDF content-type blocked; quote owed on a session that can read them). The founder's
52% is graded **contested-essay-family, ~3.5× the assessment consensus — unusable as a
claim**; the honest version of the founder's point (livestock is a first-order emissions
lane that hemp products can displace) survives at 12–14.5% without the inflation.

## 6 · WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY

The founder's economic thesis does not live or die on these rows: the arbitrage lane
($400/kg vs $100,000/kg distillate; $0.08/kg closed-loop production) is cost arithmetic,
and the displacement lane (textiles, pulp, lumber, insulation substituting
petro/wood/livestock products) is exactly where the receipted science says the climate
case actually lives — in the products and what they displace, on hundred-year horizons,
with yield as the uncertainty to measure. That is the version the university teaches.
