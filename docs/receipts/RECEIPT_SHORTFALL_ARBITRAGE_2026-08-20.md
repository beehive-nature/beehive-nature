## 1 · THE HEADLINE — the designation is absent from the current edition

The **Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025–2030** (10th edition, January 2026, in force
today) contains **zero occurrences** of the phrase *"public health concern"* — verified by
exhaustive text extraction of all 10 pages, not by search.

Its own companion **Scientific Foundation** report records the Advisory Committee's
recommendation to carry the designation forward — *"Highlight the existing special
considerations of nutrients and dietary components of public health concern. Calcium,
potassium, vitamin D, and dietary fiber are underconsumed…"* — and scores it
**☒ Partial**. That is the only occurrence of the phrase in the 90-page report.

**State it precisely: the phrase is ABSENT from the current edition and the
recommendation to carry it forward was scored Partial.** Do not write "abolished" or
"repealed" — the documents make no such statement, and overstating it would hand critics
the win.

and it is entirely Axis A: a comparison of one government table against another.

## 3 · THE HONEST BRAKE — carry this or the claim is a trap

**"Below the EAR" is an intake-adequacy statistic, not a deficiency rate.** NIH ODS:
by serum 25(OH)D (NHANES 2011–2014), only **5% were at risk of deficiency and 18% at risk
of inadequacy** — against 96% below the dietary EAR — because *"the FNB based the vitamin
D RDAs on the assumption that people receive minimal sun exposure."* Vitamin E intake
estimates *"might be low… because the amounts and types of fat added during cooking are
often unknown"*, and **choline has no EAR at all** (*"Insufficient data were available"*).

Publishing 96/88/74% without these three caveats would be **technically accurate and
substantively misleading** — the exact failure mode this project exists to refuse. The
brake ships beside the number, always.

### §5a · THE FATTY-ACID DISAGGREGATION LAW (founder-ruled 2026-08-20)

Founder, verbatim: *"never mention 'saturated fat' without MCT Saturated fat (essential
energy better than table sugar) and saturated fat LCT nonessential may cause ACiD
exacerbation"*

**Standing display law on every surface: the surface may never render an undifferentiated
"saturated fat" total.** The category collapses two things that a body handles by
different routes, and collapsing them is the same error class as a lab summing ten
cannabinoids and calling it "total", or a panel rendering `NotMeasured` as zero.

**The Axis A ground that makes this enforceable — we already have the data.** USDA FDC
reports individual fatty acids by chain length, not just the roll-up: `4:0 6:0 8:0 10:0
12:0 14:0 16:0 18:0`. So the disaggregation is not an interpretation we add; it is a
column we stopped hiding.

| class | chain length | Axis A facts we may carry |
|---|---|---|
| **MCT** | C6–C12 (caproic, caprylic, capric, lauric) | absorbed via the portal vein direct to the liver, bypassing chylomicron packaging; rapidly oxidised; ketogenic — well-documented lipid biochemistry |
| **LCT** | C14+ (myristic, palmitic, stearic) | absorbed via chylomicrons into the lymphatic system; the class the DGA's <10% target is dominated by |

**The founder's two claims are Axis B and route to BiGen, not to the surface:** that MCT
is *"essential energy better than table sugar"*, and that LCT *"may cause ACiD
exacerbation"*. Both are effect claims; both are pre-registration-shaped and testable;
neither may be asserted on a bFood cell. The surface shows the **grams by chain length**,
cited — which is the strongest honest version of the founder's point, because the reader
sees the two classes are different quantities before anyone tells them they behave
differently.

**A nuance that matters for our own basket, stated because it cuts against a tidy story:**
coconut oil is roughly half **lauric acid (C12)** — classified MCT by chain length, but
absorbed substantially like an LCT via chylomicrons rather than the portal route. So the
house's own MCT source is the boundary case, and the surface must show C12 as its own row
rather than folding it into either class. Hiding that would be exactly the tidy-story
failure we refuse in others.

**Gate FA-1:** the BiGen pre-registration for the MCT-vs-LCT effect thesis, alongside the
cannabinoid protocol (CB-1). **Gate FA-2:** confirm the display law's rendering — three
rows (MCT C6–C10 · lauric C12 · LCT C14+) rather than two, per the nuance above.

## 6 · A NEW SHORTFALL CREATED BY DEFINITION, JANUARY 2026 — no data changed

The 2025–2030 DGA sets a protein goal of **1.2–1.6 g per kg body weight per day**.
Measured mean intake: **1.25 g/kg men 19+, 1.09 g/kg women 19+**. The average adult
American woman is now below target — while only **9%** fall below the EAR (0.66 g/kg).
**Same NHANES, different bar.** This is the cleanest demonstration in the whole dataset
that a "deficiency" can be manufactured by moving a definition, and it belongs on the
surface precisely because it cuts against easy alarm.

*Note for the bFood build, stated so no seat conflates them:* the DGA's 1.2–1.6 g/kg is
**grams of protein** per kg. The founder's hardwired serving is **grams of hemp hearts**
per kg (1.25 g/kg). Different quantities that share a unit shape — the surface must never
let them touch. What IS computable and honest: 100 g of hearts carries 31.56 g protein
(USDA FDC 170148), so at the DGA's new target an 80 kg adult needing 96–128 g protein
gets roughly a quarter to a third of it from the house serving.

## 7 · THE AXIS TRAP, NAMED

**The designation is Axis A; the justification for it is Axis B — and they sit in the same
sentence in the source.** *"Calcium, potassium, vitamin D, and dietary fiber are
underconsumed"* is a fact we may carry. *"…associated with increased risk of chronic
disease"* is an effect claim that lives off-repo behind graded evidence. **Quote the first
half, stop before the second.** This is now the standing rule for every DGA/ODS quotation
on any surface.
