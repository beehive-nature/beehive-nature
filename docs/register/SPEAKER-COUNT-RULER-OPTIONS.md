# T-5 OPTIONS — the speaker-count ruler, as choices for the founder's desk

**From:** zCode-R (research/corpus seat) · 2026-08-20 · per WORKORDER_SPRINT_TO_FRIDAY lane 2.
**Status:** OPTIONS ONLY — the pick is the founder's (T-5). Nothing here is decided.
**The problem:** every count in FATHER-SET-1 is CANDIDATE-grade because Ethnologue — the
world's default source — is paywalled and therefore **fails Tier R: a stranger cannot
reproduce the number for free.** The register needs a lawful ruler.

**The shape already ruled (zCode-R's pre-shape, seconded in-tree):** the ruler is a
**register of records, not a database lookup** — per-language entries each carrying
source + date + method + macrolanguage classification, git-versioned, recomputable on a
schedule. What the founder picks below is the register's **backbone**, not a single number.

---

## The candidates

### A · Wikidata property P1098 — "number of native speakers"

**Licence: CC0** — the most permissive of any candidate; scraping, forking, and
republication are unconditional. **Tier R: passes** — live queryable by any stranger via
the Wikidata API/SPARQL, every statement carries references and point-in-time
qualifiers, and statement history is versioned with visible diffs.

**The property's best feature, demonstrated live (receipt pulled 2026-08-20):** Arabic
(Q13955) carries **three competing P1098 statements — +295,000,000 · +315,421,300 ·
+422,000,000** — each with its own reference and point-in-time qualifier. Wikidata does
not hide the disagreement; **it renders the disagreement as data.** That is exactly the
BiGen instinct: multiple sources, visible provenance, no silent resolution.

**Honest weaknesses:** crowd-maintained — many statements ultimately cite Ethnologue or
Wikipedia (derivative, so the CC0 shell can carry paywalled blood); coverage is uneven
for small languages; no native-vs-L2 distinction unless qualifiers are filled in.

### B · National censuses — the primary receipts

**Licence:** free where the polity publishes (most do): e.g. INSEE (France), ISTAT
(Italy), CSP.gov.lv (Latvia), Rosstat (Russia). **Tier R: passes when the register cites
the census URL + table + the exact question wording** — reproducible by any stranger with
a browser.

**The doctrinal fit is unique:** the register's own T-4b ruling says *representation is
self-defined by each polity* — and so is the **count**. A census is a polity counting
its own people with its own definition. Where the dock's address is "the Government of
France," the count's authority can be the same government's statistical office.

**Honest weaknesses:** every polity defines "speaker" differently (native / home
language / daily use — France and Italy do not ask the same question); census years are
staggered; **diaspora is usually excluded** (fatal for a francophonie-wide figure);
cross-country aggregation is apples-to-oranges unless each record carries the question
wording.

### C · UNESCO World Atlas of Languages — the vitality overlay

**Licence:** open access with attribution. **Tier R: partial** — the platform is public,
but reproducibility depends on its export/API surface, which is younger and less stable
than Wikidata's.

**Where it wins:** vitality and endangerment — the axis FATHER-SET-1's seed register
already cites. **Where it loses:** raw counts; the old Atlas's figures were in places
Ethnologue-derived, and the new platform's coverage of counts is uneven.

---

## Refuted / inadmissible, recorded so nobody re-proposes them

- **Ethnologue** — the default source worldwide, **paywalled: fails Tier R by
  definition.** Its figures may inform a CANDIDATE-grade note but can never be the ruler.
- **Glottolog** — open (CC BY 4.0) and excellent for classification and macrolanguage
  structure, but **carries no speaker counts by design.** Useful to the ruler as the
  *classification authority* (the macrolanguage pin), not as a count source.

---

## The two hazards any ruler must pin (already law in the register)

1. **Macrolanguage classification** — Arabic is ~380M *as a macrolanguage* while Modern
   Standard Arabic has effectively no native speakers; Hindi/Urdu swings by hundreds of
   millions on lump-vs-split. Every record states its classification (Glottolog's is the
   open pin); the gate's number reads the register's recomputed aggregate, never a
   vendor's.
2. **Native vs L2** — French is ~80M native but ~300M+ across the francophonie
   (OIF-claimed); the register records both, labelled, never blended.

## The options as a pick

- **A as backbone** (CC0, disagreement visible, schedule-queryable) **reconciled against
  B as primary anchors** where the polity publishes — is zCode-R's recommendation,
  because it matches the register's own shape: multiple receipts, visible disagreement,
  polity-defined where a polity exists. C rides as the vitality overlay regardless.
- **B as backbone** (census-first) is the strictest reading of Tier R and the deepest
  doctrinal fit — at the cost of coverage gaps for exactly the diaspora-rich and
  stateless languages the corpus most cares about.
- **A alone** is the cheapest to run tonight and the weakest at the edges.

**The choice — A+B, B+A, or A alone — is gate T-5's, and it is the founder's word.**
