# Swanky popsicle circuit PSI: the six RB01 observations, re-checked against upstream

RB01 is an experimental benchmark adapter. Its functional and performance results do not establish suitability for sensitive data or production use.

The constructors initialize separate RNG consumers from the same seed. The reporting seat observed overlapping streams within each party and matches between some generated bytes and protocol traffic. The security consequences remain unverified.

## Observation 6, in three layers

1. **Source-visible pattern.** At the pin, `PsiGarbler::new` passes
   `RNG::from_seed(seed)` to the swanky-twopac `Garbler`
   ([`garbler.rs#L37`](https://github.com/GaloisInc/swanky/blob/409d1ceb0831e2de11eb8da1b8f961f59a8b5276/edge/popsicle/src/psi/circuit_psi/garbler.rs#L37))
   and stores a second `RNG::from_seed(seed)` as the base-PSI rng
   ([`#L39`](https://github.com/GaloisInc/swanky/blob/409d1ceb0831e2de11eb8da1b8f961f59a8b5276/edge/popsicle/src/psi/circuit_psi/garbler.rs#L39)).
   `PsiEvaluator::new` does the same
   ([`evaluator.rs#L36`](https://github.com/GaloisInc/swanky/blob/409d1ceb0831e2de11eb8da1b8f961f59a8b5276/edge/popsicle/src/psi/circuit_psi/evaluator.rs#L36),
   [`#L41`](https://github.com/GaloisInc/swanky/blob/409d1ceb0831e2de11eb8da1b8f961f59a8b5276/edge/popsicle/src/psi/circuit_psi/evaluator.rs#L41)).
   swanky-twopac's own `Garbler::new` instead seeds its inner garbler from a
   fresh draw
   ([`twopac/src/semihonest/garbler.rs#L45`](https://github.com/GaloisInc/swanky/blob/409d1ceb0831e2de11eb8da1b8f961f59a8b5276/edge/twopac/src/semihonest/garbler.rs#L45)).
2. **Measured by the instrument** (one run, during run 1, synthetic sets of
   16 keys a side): within each party the two streams were byte-identical
   over their common prefix, 29,728 output bytes on the garbler and 4,160 on
   the evaluator, and some generated bytes matched bytes in the protocol
   traffic.
3. **Inferred consequences: none asserted.** No exploit is claimed, and the
   absence of an impact analysis is not evidence of harmlessness. The
   security consequences remain unverified.

**Where the detail goes.** The instrument, its raw output and the
per-offset detail are not in this public tree. No BNR document prescribes
how a finding about a third-party library is disclosed: `SECURITY.md` lists
third-party networks as out of scope ("report to those projects"), and its
private channel covers this repository's own security-relevant findings.
Holding observation 6's detail for Swanky's private contact is **this lane's
choice**. The pattern is security-relevant, its impact is unverified, Swanky
has no security policy at the pin, and its GitHub private reporting is
disabled. The held files are on the build box, with digests in `DIGESTS.md`.

## Informed consent at the point of use

Each reproduction prints a notice before it runs. It says:

- what the program runs, and that Swanky is research software;
- that the two parties are threads of one process on an in-process socket
  pair;
- that the data is synthetic, nothing is read from or written to disk, and
  output goes to the terminal only;
- the program's limits, including its watchdog;
- what it deliberately makes upstream do (panics, sessions that do not
  complete, or an encoding collision; the wording differs per program);
- what remains unresolved: one host and one target, behaviour elsewhere
  untested, no security property established;
- that it creates no persistent state.

The notices do not name observation 6. Every input is synthetic, so the
shared-seed pattern puts nothing of the operator's at risk in these runs.
Naming the finding there would also require the two canon statements in
every notice, and these programs are attached to upstream issues about
other defects.
